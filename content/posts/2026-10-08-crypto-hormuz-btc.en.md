There is a thread tying the Strait of Hormuz to a bitcoin wallet. On 7 October that thread pulled taut: Iranian attacks on tankers sent oil spiking, crude dragged government bond yields with it, and Bitcoin paid the bill like any other risk asset.

## Hormuz and oil: the macro trigger

During Wednesday's session Bitcoin fell as low as $82,734 on Bitstamp, its lowest level of October, down roughly 4% in 24 hours. Brent topped $102 a barrel and WTI reached 91, while ten-year Treasury yields touched their highest in 24 years. An adviser to Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps spoke of "illegal" traffic in the Strait. The technical picture is clear: with the 21-day moving average around $83,850 broken, the next support sits at 80,000. And yet the backdrop stays ambiguous: Bitcoin had just closed its best quarter in years.

## Vitalik Buterin and the risk coming from AI

While the price slips, the deeper debate is about cryptography itself. Replying to Ethereum researcher Justin Drake, who had invoked "bunker mode", Vitalik Buterin wrote that he would not recommend anyone rush to move funds into new wallets — but that the risk of AI-accelerated mathematics weakening cryptography should be taken seriously. Not only the kind vulnerable to quantum attack, but also what we today consider post-quantum: Buterin estimates a "good chance" that the concrete security of lattices takes serious hits within the next two years of AI mathematics. The operational advice is cautious: keep funds on addresses never used, but don't make rushed migrations, which have already done their share of damage.

## Stablecoins and rules: the week of infrastructure

On the regulatory and industrial front the week is dense. Hong Kong confirmed it intends to present by the end of 2026 the bill introducing licences for four activities: trading, custody, advisory and management of digital assets. Kazakhstan signed a memorandum with Tether and the Alatau City authority to study a stablecoin tied to the tenge and the tokenisation of real assets through the Hadron platform. On the industrial side, Samsung will bring USDC transfers on Solana into Samsung Wallet for US Galaxy users — 82 million devices, starting in the last week of October. Paxos added XRP to its regulated brokerage platform, and Polygon extended support to TRON in its Open Money Stack. Robinhood, finally, put 25 million dollars of bitcoin on its own balance sheet, its first declared proprietary purchase.

## The price and the ground beneath it

Bitcoin below $83,000 is the story of the day, but not the one that will last. While the market reacts to Hormuz, the infrastructure moves ahead: stablecoins in consumers' pockets, licences in regulators' drawers, and a cryptographic question that AI has just made urgent.
