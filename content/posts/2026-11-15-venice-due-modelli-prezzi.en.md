Anyone watching only the VVV price misses the most interesting part of the week. Venice's official changelog records two additions on November 14 in a single day: Gemini 4 Hydrogen, at 1.875 dollars per million input tokens and 9.375 for output, and Seedance 2.5 Flash, the video model in early access, at 0.0546 dollars per second in the image-to-video version and 0.0571 in text-to-video. Two days earlier Gemini 4 Carbon had landed, at 2.50 and 12.50, and Pareto, at 1.00 and 4.00. On November 9, on the top step, Claude Fable 5.2 at 8 and 40.

## Two speeds on the same platform

The sequence says more than any announcement: Venice is building a price ladder, not a trophy cabinet. Hydrogen serves the jobs that don't need Carbon; Pareto sits below both; Fable and Carbon cover the hard work. For an API that lives on volume the move makes sense: the margin isn't in the expensive token, it's in the number of tokens. And it is consistent with a platform that has made anonymous access its commercial argument.

## The token and its opposite

In the same hours VVV traded around 19.3 dollars according to DefiLlama and 19.55 according to CoinGecko, with a market cap between 943 and 957 million: roughly 7 per cent up over 24 hours, but still around -20 per cent on the month and down on the week as well. Staking, which DefiLlama keeps as a separate series rather than inside TVL — which stays at zero by methodology — is worth 617.98 million dollars, 65.55 per cent of the market cap. Two thirds of the circulating token are locked up producing inference; the price that moves is the price of the third left liquid.

## The burn as a shadow of revenue

On November 9 Venice burned 21,657 VVV for 458,200 dollars, the largest discretionary cycle so far, +10.9 per cent on October's 413,230. On-chain fees tracked by DefiLlama already show 804,607 dollars in November, against 1.06 million for the whole of October. Watch for a distinction the monthly figure hides: of the 33.96 million VVV burned in total, 99 per cent is a one-off event from March 2025 — airdrop and team buyback — while recurring burns are worth about 354,000 tokens. The burn is the on-chain shadow of revenue, not revenue: subscriptions, API and DIEM settle off-chain.

## Conclusion

Venice is doing two opposite and complementary things at once: making intelligence cheaper at the margin and more expensive in market cap. If the price per token falls while volume rises, the sum only works if the platform grows enough to compensate. A 7 per cent bounce in a market losing 15 per cent is a signal, not a thesis: the thesis is in the price list.
