VVV trades around 20.8 dollars, with a market capitalisation just above one billion. Against the all-time high of 34.53 dollars touched on 21 September, the token has lost almost 40%; over the past week the decline is about 7% according to DefiLlama, over the month it exceeds 15%. These are numbers that age within hours; what stays stable is the structure underneath.

## The figure the report gets wrong every time

DefiLlama's page for the Venice protocol reports a TVL of zero dollars, and it is not a reading error: it is the methodology. The aggregator keeps staking as a separate series and warns that it is not part of TVL. The value locked in the sVVV contract on Base is 721.13 million dollars, 70.39% of market capitalisation: almost three quarters of the token's value lives in a single contract, not in liquidity pools. Calling that figure "protocol TVL" is the shortcut that produces billion-and-a-half numbers. It does not exist: they are two different measures, and the second is called staking.

## Fees as the only on-chain trace of the business

DefiLlama's "Fees" field for Venice, by its stated methodology, is the dollar value of the VVV bought back and burned: subscriptions, API and DIEM credits settle off-chain and remain excluded. The series is therefore the shadow of revenue, not revenue. October marked 1.06 million dollars, September 833 thousand, August 697 thousand; the last 30 days are worth 649,005 dollars and the cumulative total 4.13 million, for an annualised rate of 4.52 million. On a market capitalisation above one billion, the implied multiple exceeds 225 times annual fees.

## What VVV's price actually measures

The official burn tracker reports 33.9 million VVV burned, 41.8% of the base the tracker itself uses, with 24.6 million staked and 8.4 million locked. These are items that do not form a clean partition and must be read as the source gives them. The point, though, is another: Venice is not a decentralised finance protocol with liquidity in pools, it is an access token to inference capacity. Whoever buys it is not funding a market, they are reserving a right of use; whoever holds it still does so because staking grants access, not protocol yield.

Comparison with lending or DEX tokens does not work, and a TVL of zero is not a verdict: it describes a different product. The real tension remains, and no chart resolves it. About a fifth of the base the tracker uses stays outside staking and locks: marginal demand for access is already satisfied, and for the price to rise someone has to pay to reserve future capacity, not present capacity. It is a bet on growing usage, not on locked value. Whoever reads DefiLlama's second column instead of the first understands that sooner.
