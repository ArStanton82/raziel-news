In the weekly report from VeniceStats on week 46 there are two companies inside the same one. One sells API credits at a pace never seen since the channel existed, that is since July. The other sells subscriptions and, for five weeks, hasn't moved. The interesting datum isn't the record burn: it's the shape of the growth.

## The channel that grows

This week the credits channel burned 22,526 VVV, which by VeniceStats' calculation imply about 2.25 million dollars of API credit purchases. The pace is about 3,218 burns a day, the highest ever for that lane, with five consecutive increases from the 2,232 a day of week 39. Credits are worth 82% of observable revenue, estimated at about 321,800 dollars a day. These are one-off purchases, not recurring ones: real revenue, but not an annuity.

## The channel that doesn't

Subscriptions added 21,357 new sign-ups (+1.2%), about 3,051 a day. It looks like a recovery, but it is a plateau: five weeks between 21,100 and 22,200, after the climb from 14,560 in week 39. The mix stays crushed into Pro (92.4%), and the average contribution per new subscriber is 2.28 dollars. Adding subscribers at this pace doesn't move the burn needle: most of the fuel comes from those buying credits.

## The burn, measured

November's discretionary cycle removed 21,657 VVV for 458,173 dollars, 10.2% more in dollar terms than October (415,719 dollars), against a budget of 555,096 USDC — 2.06× October's — at an average cost of 22.66 dollars per token. Cumulative fees on DefiLlama, which by methodology count only VVV bought back and burned and exclude subscriptions, API and DIEM, stand at 4.88 million dollars. For scale: emissions in force are 2 million VVV a year, about 167 thousand a month, and the monthly burn absorbs around 13% of them.

## The price and the noise

VVV travels around 23 dollars (23.32 on CoinGecko, 23.37 on DefiLlama at the time of reading), with a market cap of 1.14 billion. According to 10xResearch it was the only one of the twenty tokens it tracks to rise in the week of the sell-off. TVL stays at zero by DefiLlama methodology, which keeps staking as a separate series: 778.84 million dollars, 68% of the market cap. The narrative did the rest: on 17 November Arthur Hayes put VVV among his «four horsemen» with a 40-dollar target, while declaring his conviction «so-so», a small or medium position and no deadline.

A token that burns 13% of what it issues, with revenue that depends on one-off credit purchases and a retail base that has stalled, is neither the buyback machine of the press releases nor the collapse of the critics. It is a business in transition, which the market is pricing on the promise. The next number to watch isn't December's burn: it's whether subscribers climb out of the 21-22 thousand band.
