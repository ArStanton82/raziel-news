Some platforms sell a model, others sell a tempo. Venice's September changelog belongs to the second category: 267 entries in thirty days, roughly nine a day, across 57 model changes, 94 new features, 45 API updates and 71 fixes. Read as a list, it is a technical bulletin; read as a strategy, it says something more precise about how Venice intends to compete.

## A month of models

The September catalogue expanded in every direction. On text came, among others, GPT-6.1 Sol, the fast variant of Claude Opus 5.5, the omnimodal model MiMo-V2.6-Flash with a one-million-token window, and Aion 3.5. On the creative side, Seedance 2.5 and Wan 3.0 Prime Pro for video, ElevenLabs TTS v4 and Music v2.5 for audio and voice, GPT Image 2.5 in the Flare and Sunburst variants for images. Nine models were deprecated and replaced — a sign that the catalogue is pruned, not merely inflated. Alongside the models, the features: multi-angle Video Studio, voice changer, Agent chat open to everyone, Minds in beta, iOS with background generation.

## Abliterated Large V2, the nuance that matters

On 2 October the catalogue gained an addition consistent with the platform's identity: Abliterated Large V2, Abliteration AI's version of GLM 5.3 with the refusal directions removed from the weights, designed for exploit analysis, red-teaming and threat modelling. The interesting detail is not the marketing "no refusals", but the level of privacy it runs under: the anonymised one, where prompts are neither stored nor used for training. Venice has long drawn a distinction between "anonymous" and "private" models, and this choice — pairing an unfiltered model with a guarantee on the data — is more significant than any single announcement.

## The price in the background

Underneath the changelog runs the financial part. From 1 October VVV's annual emissions fell to 2 million, the last step of the plan announced in August, while the programmatic burn keeps buying and burning tokens with $5 of every $100 spent on API credits. The market, meanwhile, is not rewarding it: VVV trades around $27.5, with a market capitalisation of about $1.33 billion. The contrast is instructive. The platform adds a model a day; the token, to justify its deflationary thesis, needs those updates to translate into revenue.

Speed is a defensible strategy, but it is also a bet: whoever runs has to hope that the sum of many small novelties is worth, in the eyes of those who pay, as much as one big leap. For now Venice has chosen not to have to choose.
