On Monday November 9 Venice sent 21,657 VVV to the null address on Base, in a single transaction. At the moment of the burn they were worth about 458,000 dollars: it is the largest discretionary cycle ever executed, nearly triple June's 162,000 dollars and 10% above October's 414,000. Bankless reconstructed the series and noted that the monthly burn has grown by an average of 19.4% since December 2025. And yet the token was trading around 19 dollars, down double digits over 24 hours, with a market cap just above 926 million. The record is there. The signal is more complicated.

## A correct, small number

With 48.9 million VVV in circulation, 21,657 tokens withdrawn are 0.04% of supply. The comparison that matters is not with October but with issuance: Venice mints about 2 million VVV a year for stakers, roughly 167,000 a month. The record burn covers about an eighth of that production on its own. Deflation is a direction, not a state: it becomes a state only if burns from subscriptions and API credits grow enough to outrun the flow going out. That is exactly the variable the market is pricing, with a negative verdict for now.

## Why "zero TVL" is not a mistake

Some radars still report a TVL for Venice above a billion. On DefiLlama Total Value Locked is 0 dollars, and not because of a bug: the page warns that the staking chart is "a separate series, not part of the TVL above". Staking is worth 660.77 million, 71.34% of market cap; the inflated figure in circulation is in all likelihood staking added to something else. Venice is not a liquidity protocol: it sells inference and uses the token as an access key. Measuring it by TVL is like measuring a publisher by the square metres of the print shop.

## Revenue that shows up only as ash

The most solid series for talking about buybacks is DefiLlama's fees, which by declared methodology are the dollar value of the VVV bought back and burned: 622,340 dollars over 7 days, 1.13 million over 30, 4.69 million cumulative. November is already at 712,373 dollars, against 1.06 million in October and 833,000 in September. But the same methodology excludes subscriptions, API and DIEM credits, which settle off-chain: the burn is the on-chain shadow of revenue, not the revenue.

## Cadence as an advantage

On the product side the pace does not slow: on November 9 Claude Fable 5.2 arrived at 8 and 40 dollars per million tokens, 20% less than the previous version; on November 10 Seedream V5.5, up to 2K, from 0.0255 dollars per image. The catalogue lists 407 models across 278 families. Distribution is Venice's real moat, more than the burn.

One question remains that the numbers do not close: if on-chain revenue grows 19% a month and the token loses 12% in a day, the market is not disputing the buybacks. It is disputing the multiple it is willing to pay for a tonne of VVV issued every month.
