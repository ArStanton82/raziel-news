Three stories in one day, with a thread tying them together: distribution runs faster than regulation. A network of German savings banks switches on crypto trading in its app, a Japanese company buys an American listing to put 2,100 bitcoin inside it, and two public consultations close without a final rule in existence. Bitcoin, meanwhile, sits at 82,000 dollars, roughly a quarter below its level a year ago.

## The Sparkasse drawer

The story that bounced around social media was "40 million customers will be able to buy Bitcoin". Reality is narrower. On 19 October the Sparkassen network began rolling out crypto trading in the app: five assets — Bitcoin, Ether, XRP, Solana and Polygon's POL — no stablecoin. But access depends on the individual savings bank: the network is made of independent institutions, and only those that signed up offer the service. Execution is by DekaBank, custody goes through the Stuttgart Stock Exchange; the industry association expects 30-40 more banks each month, with about 230 interested.

The potential is enormous — the network serves between 40 and 50 million customers — but today those who can press "buy" are a fraction. In Germany bitcoin held for more than a year is tax-exempt for individuals: more than the app, that is what makes the move significant.

## Superplanet, meaning a listed shell

Super League Enterprise shareholders approved the deal with Metaplanet on 19 October: 2,100 bitcoin (about 132.1 million dollars at announcement) plus 2.5 million in cash, in exchange for 44,859,400 shares at 3 dollars. On closing, expected in the fourth quarter, the company becomes Superplanet (SUPA), a consolidated subsidiary of Metaplanet holding 95.7% of the common stock (93.6% counting the warrants). Group treasury reaches 43,000 BTC. Nothing is final until the deal closes.

## The deadlines have passed, the rules have not

On 20 October the SEC comment period on "Regulation Crypto Assets" closes, the August proposal for an offering regime dedicated to investment contracts on crypto assets: about 140 submissions in sixty days, two exemptions (one for startups of 5 million over four years, one a two-tier structure up to 75 million), a safe harbour contested by those who doubt the SEC's authority to close out investment-contract status. The day before, the Treasury consultation on payment stablecoins had closed, 87 questions. No final rule exists: the statutory July deadline was missed, the GENIUS Act becomes operative on 18 January 2027, and if the OCC publishes its rule in November — as it stated in August — the 120-day clock would push effectiveness to March 2027.

On perpetuals the CFTC filed a brief on Friday to have CME declared lacking standing: allegations of greater competition, without an imminent financial injury, are not enough. It is the dispute that will decide whether perpetuals are futures or swaps. No ruling.
