US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, after 0.6% in September. Core, stripping out food and energy, rose 0.3% for the third consecutive month: it is not coming down. And the day before publication, on November 10 in London, governor Christopher Waller had already covered himself.

## The condition that came true

In his speech to the Bank of England Watchers Conference, Waller explained why he voted to hold rates, but described himself as "inclined to support a 25 basis point increase" in December. Then came the line that travelled: if October core had stayed around 0.3% or higher, "a 50 basis point increase should be considered". The figure landed exactly there.

Three qualifications. Waller spoke before publication: he did not comment on the data, he set a threshold. These are one governor's personal views, and the committee decides. And the BLS could not publish twelve-month changes for most series, lacking the October 2025 data: the annual estimates in circulation are not official. Bitcoin, meanwhile, slid to around 73,500 dollars, an intraday low of 72,744, down 5% on the week: an asset that pays no yield now competes with securities that do.

## MARA sells so it doesn't have to sell

The story that best captures the mood of the moment is corporate. In the quarterly letter filed on November 9, MARA disclosed that after September 30 it sold 13,597 bitcoin at an average price of about 81,039 dollars, taking in around 1.1 billion. Of that, 750 million repaid bitcoin-backed loans, freeing 17,477 coins held as collateral.

The average price, 81,000 dollars, is above today's 73,500: anyone who reads every sale as a bearish signal should explain that one. The reason, though, was not a forecast, it was debt, cut by 1.4 billion since the start of the year. Revenue in the quarter fell 35% to 164.2 million, with a share buyback authorised up to 500 million.

## The banks build, DeFi counts the damage

On infrastructure the movement is slow, and therefore significant. Twenty-one institutions — Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, Santander, Deutsche Bank, BBVA among them — have committed about 270 million to Project Faro, which aims to issue stablecoins anchored to G7 currencies: the dollar in the first half of 2027, the euro to follow. Faro enters a market worth roughly 290 billion where USDT and USDC account for 85%; several members also sit in rival consortia such as Open USD and Qivalis.

On the other side, Initia confirmed on November 11 a second exploit in less than three weeks: 3.27 million taken through a command injection on a compromised Google Cloud key, fraudulent withdrawals validated on five rollups, 2.16 million still frozen on the halted network. No INIT tokens were drained: the security of a chain is now a question of cloud credentials.

Two speeds, in short: the banks arrive with five years of budget, DeFi pays the bill in three weeks.
