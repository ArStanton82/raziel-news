There are two ways to measure Venice this week, and they do not tell the same story. The first is the token price, which is falling. The second is the Roxy Cinema in New York, where on Monday the films of a festival Venice funded were screened — a festival that pays its winners in platform credits. Neither story is false. It is just that one of them refreshes every minute.

## The number going down

VVV trades around $22.1, against $34.5 at its September all-time high: roughly 36 percent lower. CoinGecko and CoinPaprika, independent sources, agree — $22.15 and $22.14, market cap around $1.08 billion, both off about 2 percent in the last twenty-four hours. With spreads that tight, it is worth stopping at the dollar and not the cent.

It is also worth saying what is not there. In recent days a figure of one and a half billion in "value locked" circulated next to these numbers: on the DefiLlama page Venice's TVL is zero, because the methodology counts tokens locked in liquidity contracts, and there are none here. Staking is a separate series and stands at $772.68 million, 69.78 percent of market cap: about 34 million VVV out of 48.7 million in circulation.

## The room filling up

The festival is called Lumara, it is presented by MoonPay and Venice, it drew more than 700 submissions and brought six finalists to New York. The prize money is not symbolic: $32,000 and 3.2 million Venice credits for the Grand Prize, $20,000 and 2 million credits for the Jury Award. "Candy" by Jiaze Li won, the jury chose "Mianmian" by Yingjie Chen, and the Serenissima award for the best film made with Venice went to "Black Thunder" by Ryan Lightbourn. Seven awards, and a digital world premiere on 30 October: 74 minutes streamed on X.

It is marketing, of course. But it produces verifiable artifacts: films that exist, a website with the jurors' names, a room in Manhattan. A token falling 36 percent says nothing about any of that.

## The burner, without adjectives

DefiLlama credits Venice with $1.04 million in fees over the last thirty days and $3.98 million cumulative; VeniceStats, an independent tracker, reports $3.98 million spent since November 2025 — the two figures match. The last discretionary cycle, on 7 October, burned 16,124 VVV at $25.72: $414,710. The next one is expected on 8 November.

Two caveats. DefiLlama's "fees" field measures only the on-chain buyback: subscriptions, API and DIEM settle off-chain and do not appear there. And the revenue estimates of $177 million a year circulating on trackers are models, not corporate disclosures.

Price measures expectation; films measure what was done. This week the two moved in opposite directions, and only one of them can be checked on demand.
