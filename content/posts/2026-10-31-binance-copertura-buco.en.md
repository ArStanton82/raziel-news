On Friday Binance did the thing an exchange does when it has just lost six billion dollars of client money: it put its own on the table. The figure is $6.34 billion. It is a real story and an incomplete story, and the distance between the two sits inside one word: custody.

## The bill, as Binance tells it

Five of the $6.34 billion are already held in the same tokens that were stolen: "the assets are in custody at Binance," a spokesperson told Cointelegraph, without naming the wallet addresses, without saying when those assets were acquired, without saying which tokens are still missing or how much of the haul has been recovered. The holdings will show up in the next proof of reserves. Binance notes that the weekly reports on its own funds to the FSRA in Abu Dhabi are an obligation that predates the hack.

Outside the fund there remain roughly $3 billion frozen with the help of partners: about $200 million at Ripple, some thirty million at World Liberty Financial across stablecoins and its own token. Co-CEO Richard Teng named a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop as the vector, and denied any leak of private keys.

## The hole that remains

Independent verification stops earlier. CertiK's head, Ronghui Gu, estimates that the $3 billion frozen plus the $1.17 billion emergency fund cover about 66 percent of the losses, leaving a gap of $2.1 billion: 1.3 percent of the $165 billion in declared reserves. CertiK adds that it needs more information to confirm the claimed $5 billion on-chain; Nansen cannot see past the emergency fund.

"In custody" is an accounting assertion, not a proof: it becomes verifiable when someone publishes the numbers.

## Politics arrives after the damage

On Friday the affair reached the White House. Donald Trump said at a press conference that he was following the case and had been told by Binance that the funds would be returned. CFTC chairman Michael Selig was blunter on CNBC: "the real problem is these are offshore, unregulated exchanges, and they don't have the same protections that we have in our financial markets." On 5 October the agency proposed a federal framework for retail leveraged trading. The same day Cody Carbone, head of the Digital Chamber, wrote to the leadership of the House and Senate asking for a law that would give the CFTC authority over exchanges and separate client funds from corporate funds in the event of bankruptcy: "regulators can only act after the damage is done." The Clarity Act is still stuck in the Senate.

## The other side of the bill

Also on Friday, Tether published its third-quarter attestation signed by BDO: $1.5 billion in operating profit, $4.5 billion over the first nine months, excess reserves nearly doubled to $7.95 billion, $132.4 billion in US Treasury securities. Whoever issues earns on government paper while whoever custodies pays the bill.

The question at the end of October is not about the six billion. It is how much of the missing $2.1 billion will still be a hole when someone publishes the numbers.
