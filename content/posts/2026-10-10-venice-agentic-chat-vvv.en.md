Venice is shifting its centre of gravity. For years its selling point was a single one — privacy, no logs, no censorship — and it worked. Now the company adds a second axis: no longer just a chat you ask things of, but an agent that does them. It is a change of posture, because an agent that uses tools touches external systems, and there the promise of confidentiality has to reckon with the actions performed.

## Agentic Chat becomes the front door

According to the official changelog, Agentic Chat — internally Chat V2 — is now Venice's default experience: a multi-step mode in which search, reasoning, image and video generation and editing all happen inside the same conversation, without jumping between separate chats. The company's social messaging pushes the usual theme — "the questions that matter most are the ones you cannot risk having recorded" — but the substance is that Venice stops selling answers and starts selling workflows. It is the direction of the whole sector, and also the hardest to reconcile with privacy: delegating a task means trusting not only the model, but the tools it touches.

## Frontier at commodity prices

In the catalogue, the most concrete signal is Claude Haiku 5.5, Anthropic's fast and cheap model, now on Venice with a one-million-token context window. The platform's price list quotes it at 0.13 dollars per million tokens on input and 0.63 on output, with anonymised privacy: figures that make the high-volume use typical of agents sustainable. (The internal report cited 0.125 dollars on input: the verified figure is 0.13.) Offering third-party frontier models at these prices is the most pragmatic way to widen the base without training anything in-house.

## The token shrinks

On tokenomics, the direction is declared: from October 2026 VVV's annual emissions fall from 2.5 to 2 million tokens, after the cut already made in September. Observers close to the project, such as JonShapeShift, read it as "the march toward a deflationary VVV": more burn, less new supply. On the market the token trades around 22.7 dollars, with a market cap of about 1.1 billion. One note of caution: the protocol TVL cited in the report (roughly 1.55 billion) finds no confirmation on DefiLlama, which today shows zero.

## Conclusion

Venice is trying to do two things at once: remain the refuge of those who do not want to be tracked and become a competitive agent platform. The low-cost models and the agentic chat push in the second direction; the deflationary token helps fund the first. The tension between the two is the real ground on which what Venice will be in the coming years will be decided.
