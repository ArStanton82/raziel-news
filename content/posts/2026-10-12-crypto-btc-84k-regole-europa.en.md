Monday opens with a signal of resilience. Bitcoin is back above 84,000 dollars and holds its weekend gains, while oil retreats. Yet the previous week the spot ETFs listed in the United States had recorded 681 million dollars of outflows, the highest figure since June. For now, the market chooses to look elsewhere.

## The market shrugs off the outflows

Resilience is the most interesting data point of the day. Despite the ETF outflows, the main digital assets are advancing: nine of the ten largest by market capitalisation are up over the last 24 hours. Analysts point to 82,000 dollars as the key support to defend and to the US inflation reading on 14 October as the next catalyst. The reading is that liquidity is shifting, but not leaving: investors are looking elsewhere, they are not running away. Most of the outflows were concentrated on 7 and 8 October, a rebalancing move rather than a flight.

## ESMA: three months to exit non-compliant stablecoins

On the regulatory front, on 8 October the European Securities and Markets Authority published a supervisory opinion: providers authorised under MiCA must cease services linked to stablecoins that do not comply with the regulation, and national authorities must clean up residual exposures within three months, that is by 8 January 2027. It is not a general ban on circulation, but an operational obligation that directly affects tokens such as USDT. According to Circle, only 3 of the 30 most capitalised stablecoins comply with MiCA: USDC, USDG and EURC.

## France and the CFTC: rules in the pipeline

France is moving on two fronts: the 2027 budget bill provides for a tax on crypto-to-stablecoin swaps from 1 January 2027 and a reporting obligation for wallets worth 112,000 dollars or more. It should be said that the Finance Committee then rejected the entire tax section of the text, so the measures will have to be approved again on the floor: they are not law yet. In the United States, former CFTC acting chairman Caroline Pham argues that the agency can regulate almost everything except "onions and movie tickets", without the need for a new act of Congress.

## Security and accumulation

On the technical side, RippleX has revealed that a bug present since 2015 in the XRP Ledger could have created XRP beyond the 100 billion limit; no exploitation was detected and the patch arrived on 25 September, skipping the normal validator voting process for the first time in more than ten years. Meanwhile El Salvador has brought its reserve to about 7,801 bitcoin, fed — according to the government and the IMF — by private donations and not by public funds.

## Conclusion

Crypto is living in two times today: a market that absorbs the outflows and holds, and a regulation that advances through deadlines and obligations. The question is no longer whether the rules will arrive, but how much it will cost to adapt without losing access.
