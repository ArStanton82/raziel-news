The two dates are fifteen days apart and they describe two different legislatures. On January 3, 2027 the Congress elected on November 3 takes its seat, and with it dies everything that never became law: including the CLARITY Act, the market-structure bill the industry has been working on for more than a year. On January 18, 2027, by contrast, the OCC's final rule implementing the GENIUS Act on stablecoins takes effect. A law that may never pass and a rule that passes regardless: that is a snapshot of where American crypto regulation has moved.

## Eleven votes that aren't there

The House passed the CLARITY Act, H.R. 3633, on July 17, 2025, by 294 votes to 134. The Senate, on September 15, 2026, stalled at 49 to 50 on the procedural motion: eleven votes short of sixty. Republican Thom Tillis voted against it precisely in order to file a motion to reconsider, which remains pending and would allow the bill to return to the floor without starting over.

The Democratic victory in both chambers, as projected by the Associated Press, changes the context of that vote: whoever blocked the bill in September no longer has any reason to bargain before taking control of the committees. The useful window is a single one, between the election and the swearing-in, and the arithmetic has not moved. Prediction markets — thermometers, not oracles — put the odds of a law by the end of the year at around 6%.

## Plan B isn't a plan, it's already in force

The OCC final rule, announced on October 21 and published as 91 FR 67000, makes the pre-approval requirement for issuing a payment stablecoin in the United States operative from January 18, 2027: without permitted payment stablecoin issuer status, issuing becomes illegal and carries criminal exposure. It covers reserves, redemption, risk management, custody, capital and the registration of foreign issuers.

There is a relief valve: an issuer with a substantially complete application on file by January 18 may request a waiver of up to twelve months. The OCC will say through a separate announcement when it starts accepting applications, because the forms are waiting on OMB clearance.

## What's left of the legislative year

The irony is that the CLARITY Act was mainly meant to draw the boundary between the SEC and the CFTC — that is, to solve a problem the agencies are already handling through coordination. If the bill dies on January 3, the sector is not left without rules: it is left with rules written by people who do not answer to the ballot, and with the prospect of rewriting them after 2027.

For an investor the difference between a statute and a rule is not theoretical: a statute sets the perimeter for years, a rule changes with a press release. The paradox is that the American market risks getting its most solid framework — the one on stablecoins — out of technical execution, and losing the most political one.
