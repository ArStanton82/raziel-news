A physically backed ETP on the token of a private artificial intelligence company, listed on the Zurich stock exchange, with bank custodians and institutional market makers. It is the kind of news that seems to close a circle: crypto turned into an orderly financial product. Then you read the numbers and the circle opens again.

## What was listed

On 20 October 21Shares AG announced two physically backed products: the Lighter ETP (LIHT) on Xetra and the Venice Token ETP (VENC) on the SIX Swiss Exchange. The issuer's factsheet says: annual fee of 2.50 percent, ISIN CH1614108301, listing on 19 October, 65,000 securities outstanding, an initial NAV of 20 dollars per unit, for assets of roughly 1.3 million.

Custodians: Anchorage Digital Bank and BitGo. Legal structure: debt instrument, Swiss domicile, eligible for UCITS and AIF.

## The token has no claim on revenue

The same day Tokenomist Research published an analysis of the burns on Base. Venice's burn rules are fixed and public — 2 dollars on an 18-dollar Pro, 5 on Pro+, 10 on Max, 5 percent of every API credit purchase — so every payment leaves a trace on the chain.

From 1 to 17 October the burn wallet recorded 93,837 payments: about 336,000 dollars a day, 123 million a year. Eighty-one percent are API credits, not chat subscriptions. Erik Voorhees had declared revenue above 150 million a year on 14 October: the chain neither confirms nor denies that figure, it says the order of magnitude is that one.

In October the burns channelled toward the token about 10 percent of the declared figure and 12 percent of the one estimated by Tokenomist. The automatic burn accounts for roughly 6 percent of sales; what goes beyond that depends on a discretionary decision by the company. VVV grants no right to revenue or to equity: the link runs through a rule the owner can change.

## Programmed scarcity, and its limits

Annual issuance has fallen 86 percent, from 14 million tokens at the January 2025 launch to 2 million from 1 October 2026. That is the main reason the burns cancel about a quarter of the new flow.

But the 21Shares factsheet says it without mincing words: recurring burns remain below issuance and revenue is not certified. At October's pace, a year of burns is worth about 1 percent of market capitalization. The token is priced as if it were the company, and it is not.

## The market, and the real question

VVV trades around 26 dollars, for a market capitalization of about 1.27 billion according to CoinGecko; the product listed in Zurich is worth 1.3 million, three orders of magnitude below. For now VENC is an access experiment, not an inflow.

The question that remains has nothing to do with Switzerland: when a traditional investor buys an ETP, is he buying Venice's business or only its burn policy? They are two different things, and the second is revocable.
