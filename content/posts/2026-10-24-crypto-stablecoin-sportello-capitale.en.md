In the same week, Hong Kong put a stablecoin into the pockets of 3.3 million people and spot bitcoin ETFs handed back $635.6 million in a single day. This is not a contradiction: it is the portrait of a sector where payment infrastructure is maturing while speculation deflates.

## One bank, three million people

HSBC RedCoin, ticker RCHKD, has been live on PayMe since 23 October. It is the bank's first stablecoin, denominated in Hong Kong dollars, authorised by the HKMA under the Stablecoins Ordinance, with a licence obtained in April. More than 3.3 million PayMe users will be able to activate a wallet in the coming days and use it for peer-to-peer payments and at more than 100,000 merchants, with one-to-one redemption against the local dollar. By the end of the year the wallet will move into the mobile banking app, with the option to subscribe to tokenised products, starting with a new share class of the Hong Kong dollar money market fund.

This is the point where a stablecoin stops being an object for traders and becomes a payment account with a logo on top.

## Coordination is not yet a corridor

On the regional front, on 23 October the Monetary Authority of Singapore brought Bank Negara Malaysia and Bank of Thailand into BLOOM as policymaker members, alongside nine new industry participants including Ripple, Visa, Mastercard and the World Bank Group. The project works on three things: cross-border settlement, interoperability between digital money networks, and programmable controls on identity and sanctions.

The announcement, however, contains no common licence, no shared currency, no launch date; and Ripple remains a participant, not a designated provider. The corridor, for now, is a table.

## Capital pulls back

The market is going elsewhere. On Thursday 22 October spot bitcoin ETFs recorded $635.6 million in net outflows, the worst session since 25 June: BlackRock IBIT −$254.1 million, Fidelity FBTC −$217.5 million, ARKB −$109.4 million. The week is still positive at $442.7 million, but October has slipped back into the red by roughly $176 million.

The most telling figure comes from CME. Record quarterly revenue of $1.737 billion (+13% year on year), but in crypto average volume fell to 196,000 contracts from 250,000 in the previous quarter, with daily notional at $7.3 billion against $13.7 billion. The record is corporate, not crypto, and CME does not break out the contribution.

## What remains when the party ends

On 22 October New York sought $1.367 billion from the founders of NovaTech, following a default judgment in August 2025: $1.13 billion in disgorgement and $232.5 million in interest, on $1.16 billion in deposits collected between 2019 and 2023, of which only $26 million ever reached a broker.

The two curves — a bank distributing, capital withdrawing — do not contradict each other. They point to the same thing: value is moving from the premium paid for a gamble to the boring margin of settlement. It is a maturation, and like every maturation it looks very little like a rally.
