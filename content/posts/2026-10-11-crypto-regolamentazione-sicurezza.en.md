The week just gone tells of two speeds in crypto regulation. In Europe the push is restrictive and centred on compliance; in Asia what prevails is the controlled opening of new investment channels. In between, the United States is trying to draw sharper legal boundaries.

## ESMA: three months to exit non-compliant stablecoins

On 8 October the European Securities and Markets Authority published a supervisory opinion: crypto service providers authorised under MiCA must stop services tied to stablecoins that do not comply with the regulation. National authorities must have the remaining exposures wound down within three months, that is by 8 January 2027, allowing only the activities needed to sell, convert, withdraw or custody the assets. It is not a general ban on circulation, but an operational obligation for intermediaries that directly touches tokens such as USDT.

## Thailand: Bitcoin and Ether ETFs from 16 October

On the opposite front, the Thai SEC has finalised the rules for crypto ETFs, in force from 16 October. At first only Bitcoin and Ether will be admitted. The funds will have to be passive, keep an average exposure of at least 80% of net assets in the tracked asset, use authorised custodians and list exclusively on the Stock Exchange of Thailand. Margin lending to buy them is prohibited.

## CFTC: the line between swaps and betting

On 9 October the CFTC issued an interim final rule excluding casino-style betting from the definition of a swap, and opened a consultation to bring certain event contracts inside the same definition. It is an attempt to clarify where federal jurisdiction over derivatives ends and where betting regulated at state level begins.

## Security: Vitalik and the XRP Ledger bug

On the technical side, Vitalik Buterin urged people not to panic about wallets but to take seriously the risk that AI will accelerate attacks on cryptography, including some lattice-based post-quantum constructions. Meanwhile RippleX revealed that a bug in the XRP Ledger, present since 2015, could have allowed XRP to be minted beyond the 100 billion limit; no exploitation has been detected and the fix arrived on 25 September.

## Conclusion

The direction is clear: crypto is entering the mesh of ordinary regulation, with harsher rules where the risk is systemic and wider doors where the investor is channelled. It is a slow and uneven integration, but it is exactly what happens when a technology stops being niche and becomes infrastructure.
