On 23 November two pieces of news arrived that fit together like two parts of the same drawing. CFTC staff published letter 26-31: bitcoin, ether and payment stablecoins can count, at a discount to their value, toward the coverage of twelve months of operating costs for derivatives exchanges and clearing houses. The same day Cross River and LendingUSA launched consumer loans backed by bitcoin. Meanwhile bitcoin was sliding toward 60,000.

## The letter: eighty cents on the dollar

The document is explicit and should be read in its technical wording. The Market Oversight and Clearing and Risk divisions do not object if a regulated market applies the same haircuts already set for the capital of brokerage firms: 20% minimum on bitcoin and ether, 2% on payment stablecoins, 100% on any other crypto-asset that is not readily liquid, which therefore cannot be used. A dollar of bitcoin is worth eighty cents of reserve. There is a substantial limit: these assets are not «cash» nor highly liquid securities under accounting principles, so they stay outside the separate liquidity requirement, which must be covered in cash or securities. It is a staff opinion, not a rule: it creates no rights and no obligations.

## The bank: up to 250,000 dollars

On the other side of the same idea, retail credit. LendingUSA's new lines, originated by Cross River — an FDIC member institution — run from 10,000 to 250,000 dollars, with a loan-to-value chosen among 30%, 50% and 70% (70% only with the interest-only formula), interest-only repayment or no monthly instalments, no early repayment penalty and collateral held by a qualified third party. The product is not available in every state, and the risk note in the release is frank: if bitcoin falls, the loan may require more collateral or its liquidation.

## The context that makes the news bitter

The timing is everything. Bitcoin is worth 60,232 dollars, 6.4% less in a week, 24.9% less in thirty days. US spot ETFs recorded 1.79 billion dollars of net outflows in the week to 20 November, the fourth consecutive and the third largest since they exist; November has passed 4.1 billion, just below the June record. The infrastructure arrives while the price gives way, which is how infrastructure has always arrived in this market: after the decline, not before.

The number to keep is 20%. It is not a rhetorical concession: it is the official price of the risk of holding bitcoin on an intermediary's balance sheet, and it is consistent with what a bank asks of an individual — never more than 70% of the value, third-party custody, possible liquidation. An asset becomes money when someone agrees to lend against it, and becomes infrastructure when the rules assign it a stable discount. We have moved to the second stage with the price falling: the collateral exists, so does the credit, liquidity remains something else.
