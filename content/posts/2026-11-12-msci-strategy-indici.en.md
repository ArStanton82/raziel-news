On Wednesday evening, after the US close, MSCI published its November review. In the final list of affected companies there is one line that closes months of discussion: «STRATEGY A», action «Deleted». Michael Saylor's company leaves the MSCI USA index and, by consequence, the World and ACWI indexes too. The changes take effect after the close on November 30.

MSTR lost 1.65% in after-hours trading. That is a modest reaction for news that had looked epochal, and the reason lies in the way the rule works.

## Five names, and one of them is uranium

Coverage tends to drop a detail: there are five deletions. Alongside Strategy go Japan's Metaplanet (from the Japan Small Cap index), treasury company Upexi (USA Micro Cap), Britain's Yellow Cake — which holds uranium, not bitcoin — and Iceland's Skel Fjárfestingafélag.

That fifth name is the key. If the criterion were a judgment on bitcoin, Yellow Cake would have no business there. MSCI instead applied a general definition of operating company, and crypto treasuries fail it because their value comes from holding an asset, not from running a business.

## The test in two steps

The mechanics are declared: it first checks whether at least half of assets are operating; anyone short of that threshold is measured against five financial ratios and becomes ineligible if they fail four. To limit turnover, a security already in the index leaves only if it fails the test in both of the last two fiscal years. SharpLink, TON Strategy and Solana Company stay on the watchlist.

Strategy contested the framework, arguing that MSCI had «injected policy judgments» into index construction, and a bitcoin think tank spoke of an «invisible committee». It is a serious objection: whoever decides what a company is holds a power no regulator was elected to.

## The bill is smaller than the headline

JPMorgan had estimated that excluding Strategy could trigger roughly 2.8 billion dollars of passive selling. The company answered with its own numbers: MSCI-linked funds held about 13 million shares, 3.1% of common stock and less than a day of trading.

An index exit is not a delisting: MSTR keeps trading on the Nasdaq, Metaplanet in Tokyo, and neither is obliged to sell a single bitcoin.

## The context that makes all of it more fragile

The removal arrives in a market that has already stopped buying. Bitcoin is worth around 73,500 dollars, and US spot ETFs closed the week to November 6 with 1.28 billion dollars of outflows — the worst since last June — taking the net balance for 2026 to about 914 million in outflows.

A model that rested on passive demand discovers that demand can withdraw twice: through the funds that replicate a benchmark and through the investors who buy it in order to own bitcoin. MSCI has decided what a company is. The market, over the coming months, will decide what a treasury is worth.
