The figure Galaxy Research published yesterday is a headline-grabber: managers filing 13F forms with the SEC added the equivalent of 49,229 bitcoin to their US spot ETFs in the third quarter, reversing three consecutive quarters of net selling — 47,054 bitcoin sold in total — and bringing declared holdings to 356,491 BTC, an all-time high worth 29.78 billion dollars as of 30 September. Managers with a declared position rise by 191, to 2,166.

All true. With one detail the headline doesn't carry: that portrait is seven weeks old.

## Who bought, and with what money

The interesting part isn't the quantity, it's the composition. Half of the net increase comes from advisers and wealth platforms — 144,307 BTC in total, 40.5% of everything reported in the 13Fs and 11.2% of the bitcoin held by the funds. It is the ninth consecutive quarterly increase: in the three quarters when institutions were selling, this category was buying.

On the other side, hedge funds have halved: the three categories together are worth 19% of declared positions, against 50.6% at the end of 2024, and basis and relative-value funds fell from a peak of 101,826 BTC to 34,053. The marginal buyer has changed nature: less leverage churning, more advice billing commissions. It is a slower, duller shareholder base, and it is the most significant news in the report. Wells Fargo is for the first time the largest declared holder (30,553 BTC, +14,636); BlackRock weighs 65.2% with 232,453 BTC, and with Fidelity the total reaches 77%.

## The quarter is over, the market isn't

At the end of September bitcoin was worth about 83,600 dollars. Today it travels just above 61 thousand, roughly a quarter less. Galaxy writes it without beating around the bush: bitcoin held by the funds fell by 38,531 units, to 1,252,139, as of 16 November. Net outflows from US spot ETFs reached 515 million in October and about 2.9 billion since the start of November, according to the TFTC tracker: thirteen trading sessions, two in and eleven out. Farside, queried by IBTimes, arrives at a similar total for November (2.89 billion); in TFTC's table BlackRock alone accounts for about 2.4 billion of the balance.

Two independent measures converging on the same order of magnitude: in this trade, that is the maximum confirmation one can get on a flow.

## What 13Fs cannot say

It is worth recalling the limits of the source, which Galaxy itself lists: the 13F photographs long positions at quarter-end, only for accounts over which the manager has discretion; it does not see hedges or derivatives and tends to understate the wealth channel, because many accounts are client-directed.

The reasonable reading sits in the middle. The record is real but retrospective; the outflow is real but recent. The composition — advisers buying for nine quarters, hedge funds in retreat — suggests the strong hand hasn't left, but has stopped adding. Whether it is a pause or a reversal will take the fourth-quarter 13F to know.
