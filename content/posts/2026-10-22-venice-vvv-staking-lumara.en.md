The number going around is a TVL of $1.92 billion. On DefiLlama's primary page, on October 22, the Total Value Locked of the Venice protocol is zero. It is not a loading error: it is a definition. Understanding why the two statements coexist explains more about the VVV token than the price does.

## The bounce

VVV is trading around $27, with a market capitalization of roughly $1.32 billion and 24-hour volume of nearly $55 million. Over seven days it is up 21.1 percent; over thirty it is still down 12.7 percent, and the all-time high of $34.53, touched on September 21, is still 21 percent higher. The move is real, but it is a recovery inside a decline, not a change of regime.

## The metric that doesn't add up

DefiLlama keeps staking as a separate series and does not add it to TVL. The figure today's report calls TVL — $1.92 billion — does not appear anywhere on the protocol page. What does appear is a different number: $947.86 million of VVV staked, equal to 71.91 percent of the market cap.

That is a far more interesting data point than a TVL. It means nearly three quarters of market value is immobilized in a contract that pays a yield in new issuance — about 6 percent a year at the current rate — and unlocks the app's Pro features. The available liquidity is what is left. A token with this structure does not move like a stock but like a promise with an implicit expiry: staking pays as long as the issuance holds.

## The burn is not revenue

The second trap is accounting. The DefiLlama page shows $856,436 in October revenue, $985,346 over the last thirty days and $3.8 million cumulative, an annualized rate of $4.33 million. That is not Venice's revenue: the methodology explicitly excludes subscriptions, API credits and DIEM, which settle off-chain. What is measured is only the buy-and-burn in VVV.

Actual revenue is another matter, and on this the sources diverge: Erik Voorhees said on October 14 that revenue was above $150 million a year, while Tokenomist's count of burns on Base implies about $123 million. Both describe a company that is growing; neither says how much reaches the token, which has no claim on revenue or equity.

## The festival, and the audience nobody measures

Venice buys visibility where the money doesn't reach: the Lumara Film Festival, presented with MoonPay, gave the Serenissima Award to "Black Thunder" by Ryan Lightbourn, twelve minutes of science-fantasy, $12,000 and 1.2 million Venice credits. Screenings and award ceremony at the Roxy Hotel Cinema in New York on October 26.

It is marketing, but it is also the only adoption indicator that doesn't pass through a wallet. Maybe that is why CF Benchmarks devotes its weekly space to research on VVV: when benchmarks arrive, products arrive too.
