The news looks like a victory: a court grants Binance a worldwide freezing order of 6.5 billion dollars. Then you read the second line, the one almost nobody headlines. The amount is not the money recovered: it is the ceiling of the order. And the target is unidentified persons, against whom the measure can act only through those who actually hold the assets.

## What the court ordered

On 18 November the courts of the Abu Dhabi Global Market issued a worldwide freezing order against the unidentified perpetrators of the 29 October attack, together with orders requiring delivery of the stolen assets. The measure, according to the Gibson Dunn lawyers representing the exchange, can be served on the blockchain: service travels to addresses, not to people, and leans on a «live» list of 26,000 wallets updated over time. Eighteen named companies, including Circle and BitGo, must maintain the freezes already in place; the recipients will have to declare under oath the assets they hold above 50,000 dollars by 30 November, and on 1 December the court will decide whether to extend the injunction.

## A ceiling, not a haul

The next day, the filed documents add the detail that was missing: 192 crypto-assets were stolen across 11 networks. 76% of the value sits in three names — 2.50 billion in USDT, 1.42 in bitcoin, 826 million in USDC — and the stablecoins alone are worth at least 3.46 billion, 55% of the loot. Ethereum accounts for about 60% of the stolen value.

This is where the injunction shows its structural limit. An order binds intermediaries, issuers and exchanges: it works with a token that has someone able to block a balance. It does not work with bitcoin and ether, which have no issuer to summon. The sums already frozen or seized stop between 2.7 billion (Department of Justice) and 2.85 (Elliptic's estimate as reported by the Emirati press): a lot, but a piece of the total.

## The part nobody headlines

The work that matters to customers has already been done elsewhere. On 12 November Binance said it had covered the affected assets by committing more than 6.34 billion across treasury, the Secure Asset Fund for Users and insurance funds, with a new proof of reserves over 198 tokens and a pledge to restore SAFU to about one billion. Refunding customers and recovering the proceeds are two different operations: the first is accounting, the second depends on courts, on time and on how slow the hackers were.

In the meantime the company looks ahead: its Asia-Pacific head told Reuters that in 2027 it will enter five Asian markets and that no fresh capital is needed to absorb the losses. The lesson of the case is the usual one, and it stays uncomfortable: in the on-chain world, the law arrives where intermediaries exist. Where there are none, only the chain is left, and the chain does not execute injunctions.
