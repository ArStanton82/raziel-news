Price makes noise; infrastructure makes less noise but weighs more. In this window the two moved together: Bitcoin tried to break a high, while someone else was writing the rules for the market to come.

## Bitcoin: the eighth attempt

Overnight between Sunday and Monday Bitcoin climbed above 86,000 dollars, touching an intraday high just under 87,000 before falling back. This is not an isolated episode: it is the third attempt in a week to challenge the late-September high, around 87,400. On 2 October the price reached 87,229 dollars, its highest level since January, after weak jobs data pushed Treasury yields lower. Citigroup raised its twelve-month target to 113,000 dollars. At the time of writing bitcoin hovers between 85,900 and 86,400 dollars, in what remains the third consecutive week of gains.

## Wall Street that doesn't close

While the price looks for direction, the infrastructure is moving. On 4 October OKXICE — an equal joint venture between OKX and Intercontinental Exchange, the parent company of the NYSE — filed with the SEC to launch a tokenized securities venue under the new innovation exemption. The platform would start with more than 60 US-listed companies, from Nvidia and Apple to Strategy, Coinbase and Circle, with 24-hour trading and Uniswap v4 liquidity pools on XLayer. The rules are tight: a maximum of 75 securities, volumes no higher than 0.25 per cent of the daily average, an exemption valid for five years and thirty days for companies that want to object. "A historic step toward a truly global, 24/7 Wall Street," commented co-chair Andrew Cuomo.

## Paying without being recognised

On the privacy front, on 1 October the Ethereum Foundation brought zkAPI to mainnet, built with the Open Anonymity Project. You deposit ETH or USDC into a vault, and a zero-knowledge proof authorises the spend without revealing which deposit is yours. The provider sees the requests, the payment layer sees the spend, and neither links the two. The limit is stated openly: zkAPI hides neither the content of the prompts nor network metadata, so sessions remain correlatable through IP address, timing or writing style. The cryptography protects the wallet, not the conversation.

## Conclusion

Three different stories, one common theme: the crypto market is trading not today's price but tomorrow's rules. Bitcoin's rally is the most visible part; the part that matters is the slow construction of the infrastructures — and the limits — within which that price will have to live.
