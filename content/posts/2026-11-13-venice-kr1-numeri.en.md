A press release to the London Stock Exchange is an odd document for a discussion of adoption: it is written by a shareholder, not by the company. The one KR1 published on November 12 delivers the most ambitious portrait of Venice to circulate so far, and it has to be read while keeping apart what it asserts from what can be checked.

## What the release says

KR1, listed on the London Main Market, has staked its entire 275,000 VVV position, at an indicative rate of 5.74% a year recorded on November 11, declaring itself the largest listed holder with roughly 0.56% of the circulating supply. Then it moves to adoption, citing Venice's October community update: five million users, up from four million in 68 days, and a daily inference volume that in October passed a trillion tokens, against roughly 250 billion at the end of September. Alongside that, the 150 million in annual revenue cited by Erik Voorhees on October 14 and the 65 million Series A at a one-billion valuation (Dragonfly, July).

These are the numbers of a company growing fast, and they are all statements by an interested party.

## The detail that doesn't add up

Venice's About page today invites you to join "over 4 million users": that is not a denial of the October figure, it is a page nobody has updated, but it marks the distance between what the company writes about itself and what a shareholder writes about it. The same goes for the trillion tokens a day: with 150 million in annual revenue it would imply 0.41 dollars per million tokens, meaning a mix that is almost entirely cheap models. Possible, but it is an inference.

More solid is the on-chain bookkeeping. DefiLlama shows a TVL of zero dollars: by methodology the protocol holds no deposited liquidity, and staking is kept as a separate series. That series is worth 651.34 million dollars, 70.96% of a 917.89 million market cap; CoinGecko confirms the price at 18.77 dollars and the circulating supply at 48.96 million. Cumulative fees — the dollar value of the VVV bought back and burned — reach 4.73 million.

## Burning is not billing

This is the comparison that clarifies the scale: a billion-dollar valuation against 4.73 million burned since the beginning, 757,941 of it in November alone. The burn of November 9 — 21,657 VVV for roughly 458,000 dollars, the largest discretionary cycle so far — is worth about a quarter of the month on its own. It also confirms the mechanism KR1 describes: 5% of API credit purchases buys VVV back on the market, and every new Pro, Pro+ and Max subscription burns 2, 5 and 10 dollars.

The point is not that Venice doesn't work. It is that its on-chain shadow measures a fraction of the story: subscriptions, API and DIEM credits settle off-chain and leave no trace. Anyone who reads "five million users" and then sees 22,617 dollars in fees over the last 24 hours has not found a contradiction: they have found the line between what can be verified and what can only be told.
