This morning bitcoin is worth 60,232 dollars, 6.4% less than seven days ago and almost 25% less than a month ago, with US spot ETFs losing 1.79 billion dollars in the last week. Over the same interval VVV has gone from about 21 to 25 dollars: CoinGecko quotes it at 25.05 (+16.9% over seven days), DefiLlama at 25.03-25.15 (+20%). Market cap 1.23 billion. The divergence is real and it is in the tape, not in the posts.

## The post and its misunderstanding

On 24 November VeniceStats wrote: «Six days between one all-time high and the next. We call it dryness». The post does not say which metric it refers to, and the most immediate reading is the wrong one: VVV's all-time price high remains 34.53 dollars on 21 September, and the token today is 27.2% below it. If «six days» concerns the price, in short, it does not add up. Nor is the «+1,538.7% year to date» circulating on X found on either of the two market pages: CoinGecko reports +2,378.6%, but over twelve months. Two social figures, one not to be copied and the other to be attributed.

## What can be counted

The solid numbers are elsewhere. DefiLlama, consistent with its methodology, gives zero TVL and keeps staking as a separate series: 868.57 million dollars, 70.6% of the market cap. November's fees, which by definition are the dollar value of VVV bought back and burned, stand at 1.03 million today against 1.06 million for the whole of October, and 5 million cumulative. VeniceStats confirms the order of magnitude: 5.02 million dollars «spent» in total on buybacks, with the last monthly cycle on 9 November at 21,657 VVV for 458,200 dollars — average price 21.16 — up 10.9% on October. The convergence between the two independent sources is the best check available.

## The flow behind the price

The part that explains demand, however, is estimated, not certified. The VeniceStats simulator reconstructs about 74 million dollars of annual revenue from subscriptions (268,120 active subscribers according to its model), 319,290 dollars a day of observed API credit purchases and an annualised total around 204 million, with a price-to-sales ratio of 6.1. These are third-party projections built on on-chain flows and on two public anchors declared by Venice (100 million in August, over 150 in October): useful as an order of magnitude, not to be quoted as a balance sheet. The next verifiable appointment is the 8 December burn, estimated between 384 and 523 thousand dollars; the current cycle has accumulated 7,765 VVV.

The rise is a fact, its explanation is a well-documented hypothesis. In a market that is selling, a token that burns almost three quarters of its own market cap in staking and keeps buying back on the market behaves differently from an asset that depends only on price. The question neither source closes remains: are those API credits recurring demand or one-off purchases? Until we know, the +17% is a price, not a thesis.
