On November 13 Bitcoin lost 70,000 dollars for the first time since August, sliding to 67,770 dollars according to CoinDesk and to 67,555 on Coinbase according to Yahoo Finance. Ether fell to about 1,900 dollars, its lowest level since mid-August. Twenty-four hours later the price is lower still, around 66,900 dollars. This is not a sudden crash; it is a descent that ate confidence one day at a time.

## Leverage pays the bill

Liquidations over 24 hours topped 1.64 billion dollars, according to CoinGlass: 88.7 per cent on the long side, with 916 million on Bitcoin and 289 million on Ether. Yahoo Finance reports 1.65 billion with almost identical percentages, 918.66 million on BTC and 287.68 on ETH.

The point is the composition: the market is not selling, over-indebted positions are being closed. A single 31-million-dollar long on Hyperliquid was enough to trigger a cascade; futures volume rose 58 per cent while open interest fell 4.6.

## The ETFs stop buying

The macro push is well known: a stronger dollar, rising yields, expectations of a Fed hike in December. Then there is the signal that weighs more than the narrative: US spot bitcoin ETFs recorded 47.7 million dollars of outflows on Thursday, a sixth consecutive day in the red, for roughly 1.44 billion cumulated. The institutional demand that used to act as a floor has turned into an open tap running the other way. When the marginal buyer becomes a seller, the price needs only the absence of good news.

## Two answers from the regulatory front

In the same hours, policy moved two opposite pieces. The Department of Justice announced the seizure of more than 1.2 billion dollars taken from Binance on October 29, out of a haul above 6 billion attributed to the North Korean group TraderTraitor; total frozen funds exceed 2.7 billion. In parallel, the cloud infrastructure behind the "FaceStealer" scheme was dismantled — deepfakes used to steal credentials: more than 400 million in eighteen months and over 900 videos of victims.

Thailand, for its part, opened a consultation on a draft that would restrict stablecoin transfers through licensed operators: only between wallets verified as belonging to the same holder, with a ceiling of 5 million baht per day per customer and per operator, incoming and outgoing counted separately, plus Travel Rule obligations, profiling and screening against "mule" accounts and sanctioned wallets. The text is a proposal, it is not in force, and comments close on November 26.

## What remains

The market is saying that leverage was the real price of the rally, and that without ETFs buying, the bottom is not a level but a hope. Regulation is moving on two tracks: recovering what was stolen and closing the channels that dirty money passes through. Neither will lift the price tomorrow. Both say the sector is being treated as financial infrastructure, not as a bet: that is the most interesting shift of the week.
