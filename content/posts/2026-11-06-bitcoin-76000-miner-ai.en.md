5 November can be told in two ways. The first is the charts: Bitcoin below $76,000, the Nasdaq in correction, more than a billion dollars of leveraged positions liquidated. The second concerns where the energy that fed the machines computing bitcoin is moving.

## The drop and the leverage
Bitcoin went from above $80,000 to just under $76,000 in a single session, with a low below $75,500. The Nasdaq Composite lost about 5 percent, the Nasdaq 100 6.4: the worst day since March 2020, according to CoinDesk. The pretext is the midterm elections: rotation out of technology stocks and fear that a Democratic Congress will tighten the constraints on building data centers.

Leverage did the rest: about $370 million of long liquidations in the first hour, more than a billion over 24 hours, of which roughly $954 million were long, according to Bitcoin Magazine, Bitcoin Magazine Pro and CoinGlass. They were bullish bets bought on Wednesday's bounce, and they were the first to break.

## The miner changing trade
On the same day Core Scientific said something worth more than a price: "We expect to complete the exit in the fourth quarter and enter 2027 as a pure high-density colocation operator," chief financial officer Jim Nygaard said on the third-quarter earnings call. The numbers make the point: out of $232.9 million in revenue, colocation accounts for $215.4 million, about 92 percent, while bitcoin mining stops at $17.5 million, 7.5 percent.

It is not a surrender, it is a move: same power, same sheds, a different customer. The company bills 485 megawatts of colocation, with contracts for 590 MW from CoreWeave and 530 from an AMD-backed agreement. For a company like this, mining is the least profitable branch of a real estate business.

## The rules arrive anyway
While the price falls, regulation advances with precise dates. The OCC's final rule on the GENIUS Act takes effect on 18 January 2027: from then on, issuing a payment stablecoin in the United States will require federal or state authorisation, with standards on reserves, redemption and risk. Anti-money-laundering and sanctions rules stay out, deferred to a separate rulemaking with the Treasury.

Greece has meanwhile approved its first tax framework for crypto: 10 percent on annual net gains above 500 euros from 1 January 2027, with staking and DeFi income taxed as interest at 15 percent. Parliament's scientific service has warned, however, CoinDesk reports, that the wording could also hit crypto-to-crypto trades that the ministry considers non-taxable.

## The point
A market falling five percent because of data centers and a miner that stops mining to rent its sheds to AI are saying the same thing: marginal capital no longer sees bitcoin as the most interesting return, and the written rule remains the only variable nobody can liquidate.
