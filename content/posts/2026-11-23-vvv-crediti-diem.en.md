In the week ending 22 November the Venice token closed at 26.56 dollars, +30.6%, while ETH lost 5.6%. This morning VVV is worth between 25.87 (DefiLlama) and 26.33 dollars (CoinGecko), with a market capitalisation between 1.27 and 1.29 billion. The useful question is not «why is it rising»: it is what has actually changed, for real, in the accounts.

## The channel that pays

VeniceStats's weekly W47 report lines up the least-told numbers. The credits channel recorded 23,361 burns, about 3,337 a day, the highest week ever measured: by mechanism they imply 2.34 million dollars of credit purchases, and they are worth 82% of observable revenue. Subscriptions, by contrast, are stuck: 21,816 new subscribers (+2.1%), inside the 21-22 thousand band held for six weeks, with a «Pro Sub» burn of 2,315 VVV (-7.6%) for about 50,463 dollars. A company growing on one channel and not on the other.

## DIEM as a thermometer

DIEM, the inference token, is the other indicator. Utilisation rose to 53.1% from 49.4% the week before, against a quarterly baseline of 46.7%: that means 16,030 dollars a day served at list price out of 30,200 of staked capacity. Supply grew by 159 DIEM, VVV locked in sVVV by 72,980 units, to 8.60 million. The price of DIEM is 2,079 dollars, +25.9%. These are numbers that tell of inference demand.

## Burn, emissions and the ratio that matters

In November the discretionary cycle burned 21,657 VVV for 458,173 dollars, against 16,124 VVV and 415,719 dollars in October. On-chain emissions run at 2,001,370 VVV a year, about 166,800 a month: the monthly burn is therefore worth around 13% of the issued flow. On DefiLlama TVL is zero by methodology — staking is a separate series, 877.79 million dollars, 69% of the market capitalisation — and cumulative fees, that is the dollar value of the VVV bought back and burned, are 4.97 million. Orders of magnitude small against the valuation: the token is not priced on revenue, but on the promise that it will grow.

## The part that does not hold

The outside narrative is more fragile than the bookkeeping. The CoinMarketCap page that attributes the move to Arthur Hayes's «Four Horsemen» call is dated 18 November, not today. The transfer of 15,000 VVV to a wallet linked to Hayes and the «+303% buyback» circulate only in unverified posts. The «+1,538% year to date» is a social figure; CoinGecko's +30.9% over seven days is a datum, and matches VeniceStats's +30.6% for the week.

The rally is better explained by the bookkeeping than by the story: a token consumed at 13% of emissions, a credits channel growing and staking that stays two thirds of the market capitalisation. The question VeniceStats leaves open remains: are those credits stable demand or one-off purchases? Until we know, the +30% is a price, not a thesis.
