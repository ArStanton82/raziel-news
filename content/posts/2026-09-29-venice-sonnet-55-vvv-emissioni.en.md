In mid-September Venice seemed to have found its perfect narrative: the VVV token at an all-time high and a frontier model available anonymously. The following week reminded everyone that narrative and price rarely walk together.

## Sonnet 5.5, leaving no trace

On 28 September the official AskVenice account announced Claude Sonnet 5.5 on the platform, "available anonymously". The model card confirms the substance: an anonymised privacy tier, prompts neither stored nor used for training, execution in a hardware enclave, a one-million-token context window. The price is $3.75 per million input tokens and $18.75 per million output tokens. It is the same model Anthropic made available on its own API the same day, under a different label: there it is a product, here it is a product without a dossier.

## The price corrects, usage does not

VVV touched its all-time high of $34.51 on 21 September, then pulled back. On 29 September, according to market data, the token was worth about $26.31: down roughly 16% on the week and almost a quarter below the peak. Alongside the fall, the @venicestats account reported sign-ups at record highs. The figure finds no confirmation in an official source, but an account tied to Venice spoke of record daily sign-up revenue — around $406,000 — earlier in the month, while founder Erik Voorhees claimed an annualised revenue run-rate of $100 million. It is worth keeping the two things apart: the token price is one variable, platform usage another.

## Emissions: let's line the numbers up again

In recent days a claim circulated that VVV's annual emissions had been "cut by 40%, from 10 to 6 million". It is an outdated reconstruction. The official schedule — Venice's blog of 5 August 2026 and the Token Transparency Filing published on Blockworks — tells another story: 14 million at launch, then 10 (20 August 2025), 8 (23 October 2025), 6 (10 February 2026), 5 (1 May 2026), 4 (1 June 2026), 3 (1 July 2026), with a cut to 2.5 million from 1 September and to 2 million from 1 October 2026. The figure of "250 billion tokens a day" does not hold: at 3 million a year we are talking about roughly 250,000 VVV a month. On this point, the gap between what circulates on social media and what the documentation says remains the most useful piece of news.

## Conclusion

Whether the VVV/DIEM model gets imitated by other ecosystems is a suggestive market thesis, but it cannot be verified today. What can be verified is more sober and more interesting: a platform that has committed itself by contract to reducing supply, and a token that, for one week, did the opposite. The distance between those two movements is exactly the space in which an infrastructure project gets judged.
