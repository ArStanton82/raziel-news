There are days when Bitcoin's price tells you about macroeconomics rather than innovation, and days when the opposite happens. On 2 October both happened at once: the market reacted to a very weak American jobs figure, while on the infrastructure and regulatory side three pieces were moving that will matter more than a single session.

## The number that moved the market

September's employment report showed just 29,000 new non-farm jobs, against roughly 90,000 expected, with the unemployment rate rising to 4.2%. It is one of the weakest readings of the cycle: the twelve-month average was 45,000 jobs, and revisions have removed another 60,000 jobs in July and August combined. Bitcoin, already rising before the release, held above $86,000, up in the order of 2-3% over 24 hours. The most widespread reading is simple: a jobs market that cracks pushes the Fed towards a pause or a cut, not towards another rate hike. Careful, though, not to confuse cause and effect — part of the move was already under way before the data.

## USDT comes back to Bitcoin

The structural news of the day concerns the world's most widely used stablecoin. USDT, born on Bitcoin in 2014 and later migrated to Ethereum and Tron, is returning to the original network within October, through Utexo, a Tether-backed startup with a $7.5 million seed round. The infrastructure combines the RGB protocol and the Lightning network: private transfers, direct BTC-USDT swaps and Bitcoin-collateralised loans, with most transaction details kept off the public ledger. Tether's CEO, Paolo Ardoino, summed up the move with a neat phrase: USDT "is coming home". Success, however, will depend on adoption by wallets and exchanges: until then, the stablecoin on Bitcoin has nowhere to travel.

## The rules accelerate

On the regulatory front the day is dense. On 1 October the SEC proposed a custody framework for advisers and regulated funds, in certain cases allowing self-custody and recognising state trust companies as custodians. Public comment stays open for 60 days. In the background, the SEC and the CFTC continue working on joint rules after the CLARITY Act stalled in the Senate. In Europe, Circle sent the Commission a letter asking it to revise MiCA: it wants to scrap the requirement to hold 30-60% of reserves in bank deposits, replacing it with more flexible liquidity requirements. Its policy director notes that only 3 of the world's 30 largest stablecoins — USDC, USDG and EURC — are compliant with MiCA: a strong argument, but also the position of someone with an interest in changing the rules.

Euphoric market, infrastructure on the move, rules trying to catch up. Three different stories, one underlying question: how much of Bitcoin's value depends on its technology and how much, instead, on the American credit cycle.
