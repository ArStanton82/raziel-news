The weekend brought Bitcoin back above 64,000 dollars after an intraday low at 62,080: -15 per cent on the week and -22 per cent on the month, according to CoinGecko. Cointelegraph's price index, taken shortly before, showed 65,384. The point is not the last cent: it is that in seven days the market lost what it had built in three months.

## The outflows do not stop

The twelve US spot bitcoin ETFs recorded 1.07 billion dollars of net outflows last week: the second consecutive billion-dollar week, something that had not happened since late May. The negative streak runs seven sessions in a row, starting November 5, for 1.91 billion cumulated; with the previous week the tally rises to 2.35 billion over two weeks and the 2026 balance falls to -1.98 billion. On Friday BlackRock's IBIT accounted for 143.5 million, 77 per cent of the total; it holds 53.99 billion in bitcoin against 64.19 in cumulative inflows. Across the whole spot crypto ETF segment 1.55 billion left: the only inflow of the week, 227,000 dollars, came from the Avalanche funds.

## The detonator is not crypto

In The Block's account the slide began on Friday, below 69,000 dollars, while the market was digesting two pieces of news with little to do with the blockchain: Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO, announced on November 12 with Colette Kress as interim chief executive, and expectations of another Federal Reserve rate increase. It is the most interesting detail of the week: Bitcoin moved like a high-beta tech stock, not like digital gold. As long as digital gold falls when the chip supplier falls, the store-of-value thesis remains a hypothesis to be proved.

## The cracks around the edges

The rest of the sector shows its cracks. Kava Lend lost 55.2 million KAVA (3.76 million at the pre-attack price): an attacker inflated the collateral 243,000 times in a dormant BUSD market and borrowed 127 times against it. KAVA fell by almost 40 per cent. And the RWA protocol stUSDT has begun winding down, three years after a peak of 2.3 billion in TVL and with just 8.6 million left: redemptions until May 14, 2027, no public explanation.

## The window that is closing

On the regulatory front: the White House has named four commissioners for the SEC and CFTC, restoring quorums that today count two seats out of five at the SEC and a single commissioner at the CFTC. Steven Levine would take Hester Peirce's seat, Kristin Johnson Caroline Crenshaw's; at the CFTC come Nathan Anonick and Matthew Rowland, both veterans of the Clarity Act. All of them await confirmation by a Senate that in January passes to the Democrats: the window is narrow.

## Conclusion

A 15 per cent drop in a week with record outflows is not yet a capitulation: it is a market remembering that it is cyclical. The difference is that today the intermediaries are listed and the flows are public, which makes it harder to tell yourself a story other than the data.
