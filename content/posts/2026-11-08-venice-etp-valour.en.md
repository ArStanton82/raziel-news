On 5 November Valour, a subsidiary of DeFi Technologies (Nasdaq: DEFT), began trading eight ETPs in Swedish kronor on the Spotlight Stock Market. One of them is Valour Venice (VVV) SEK, ISIN CH1108681771: management fee at 1.9%, open-ended listing. The same day VVV was worth a little over twenty dollars and carried a market capitalisation around one billion, almost 40% below the high of 34.53 dollars touched on 21 September.

The two things belong together but do not say the same thing, and the word "institutionalisation" gets used for both.

## A container is not an engine

Whoever buys the ETP is not buying access to Venice: they are buying the price performance of the token inside a brokerage account, with no wallet, no keys and no staking. Whoever buys VVV on the market is buying a right of use: the token governs access to inference capacity, and that is why it gets locked up. The press release says so without euphemism, describing VVV as the token that "supports the inference capacity access model" of the ecosystem. Two distinct financial products on the same underlying, with different audiences and different mechanics.

This matters for a practical reason: an ETP that raises capital does not put a single VVV into staking and does not burn a single token. It does not increase demand for access, it increases demand for exposure. It is a difference the price may well ignore, but the protocol does not see it.

## The ledger of what actually happens

The official tracker is what tells the two apart. It reports 33.9 million VVV burned, 41.8% of the base the tracker itself uses, 24.6 million staked (30.3%) and 8.4 million locked (10.4%). Over the last twenty-four hours the ledger shows 2.73 thousand VVV burned for 59.21 thousand dollars, against 69.36 thousand the day before, a daily average of 53.91 thousand: almost all of it burned programmatically, two dollars for every Pro subscription, five for every hundred dollars of credits, ten for the Max plan, plus a discretionary burn.

These are small numbers against a capitalisation of one billion, and they should be read as what they are: the on-chain shadow of usage, not revenue.

## Meanwhile the product widens

In the same week Venice added Kling 4.0 Flash to its catalogue, Kuaishou's video model: from 0.1174 dollars per second, clips of 3 to 20 seconds with native audio, three seconds at 720p for 0.35 dollars against 0.59 for Kling 4.0 — the company announces 40% less per clip. Then GPT Image 2.6, in its two variants Flare and Sunburst, from 1K to 4K, from nine cents per image and ten per edit. The investment thesis, if a thesis exists, is here: in a catalogue that keeps widening and a price per unit that keeps falling.

An ETP on a token that burns tens of thousands of dollars a day is an experiment in how much the securities market is willing to pay for indirect exposure. The 1.9% a year is the price of convenience. The rest is decided by usage, which no listing can certify.
