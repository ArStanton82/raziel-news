Venice is working on two fronts that rarely move together: distribution and scarcity. On one side the VVV token leaves its home chain to reach a wider audience; on the other the project cuts what it issues and burns what it takes in. It is a double move that tells a precise strategy.

## The bridge to Solana

On 6 October Sunrise — the gateway of a Wormhole Labs subsidiary — announced that VVV is available on Solana, connected through Wormhole NTT and tradable on Raydium, Jupiter, Phantom and other applications. The canonical contract on the network is `VVV4UdRywr7SLafPHzqXwRCw7myXkmgCtitXUvj8g8c`. For Venice it means access to one of the most liquid ecosystems in the sector without giving up its home chain, Base. Worth noting: there is no official statement on the company blog; the news comes from the players who run the infrastructure, not from Venice itself.

## Fewer emissions, more burns

On the tokenomics side the change is substantial. Since 1 October annual VVV emissions have fallen to 2 million tokens, roughly 86% less than the 14 million at launch, after the intermediate cut of 1 September. On top of that comes a programmatic burn: 5 dollars out of every 100 spent on API credits are used to buy back and destroy VVV. Third-party observers such as VeniceStats estimate that October's buy-and-burn has passed 415,000 dollars, the largest in dollar terms so far. The total burned since inception is around 34 million tokens against a supply of roughly 81 million.

## DIEM and the cost of perpetual access

The second token, DIEM, promises 1 dollar of AI credit per day per token, with a reset at midnight UTC and no carry-over. On 10 October it traded near 1,607 dollars: at that price, recovering the cost takes about 4.4 years of full use. That is not an official Venice price but a secondary-market quote, and it should be read as such.

## Conclusion

Venice is betting that a scarcer and more widely held token is worth more than an abundant and concentrated one. The logic is sound: fewer emissions, more burns, more chains. But the real test will be whether burns can outpace emissions structurally, not merely chase them. Until then, the word "deflationary" remains a promise to be checked quarter by quarter.
