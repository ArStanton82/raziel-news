VeniceStats' W44 weekly report is titled "Red Skies Over San Marco" and tells of a week running at two speeds: VVV loses 8.8% and closes at 22.88 dollars, yet stakers add 76.6 thousand net tokens and credit purchases rise for the seventh consecutive week, to 2.18 million dollars. Price down, position up. It is not a paradox: it is the difference between those who own a token and those who use it as a key to get in.

## The number that holds

The snapshot of the day, read on the public pages, is consistent with that account. On VeniceStats, 33.54 million VVV are in staking, 66.52% of the circulating supply, with 8.35 million sVVV locked to mint DIEM — roughly a quarter of the total. The seven-day net flow is positive by 58,849 VVV with 82 new stakers, while only 82,123 VVV sit in cooldown, 0.2% of the staked amount.

The 76.6 thousand in the report cover 23-30 October; the 58.8 thousand are a rolling flow: two different windows, the same indication — at these prices someone is buying and locking up.

On price it is worth being cautious: at the time of checking, CoinGecko quotes 22.42 dollars and VeniceStats 23.15, with a market capitalisation between 1.09 and 1.14 billion. The gap is a few percentage points, enough not to publish 24-hour changes — which indeed contradict each other across sources — and small enough to trust the order of magnitude: a little above one billion.

## The TVL that does not exist

Today's report carries a "Venice TVL" of 1.589 billion dollars. That is not a data point: it is the sum of staking and market capitalisation, two things DefiLlama keeps separate by declared methodology. On the protocol page the TVL is zero, because Venice is not a liquidity protocol; the 33.5 million VVV in staking appears as a separate series, valued by the aggregator at roughly 795 million dollars, 73% of the market cap the same DefiLlama reports (1.09 billion).

Writing "TVL 1.589 billion" is not a rounding: it attributes to Venice a liquidity depth it does not have. The correct sentence is more sober and more interesting — TVL zero by definition, almost three quarters of the value in staking — and it explains why the token does not move like any other DeFi asset.

## What the week actually says

The 2.18 million in credits bought in a week are the most fragile part of the picture: they come from a community tracker, not from a press release, and they have no second independent source. They should be read as an indication, not as a certified balance sheet. The rest, by contrast, sits on-chain: two thirds of the supply locked, a growing lock for DIEM, a minimal outflow.

The lesson of the week is that for VVV the price is the noisiest variable and the least informative. Whoever buys access does not look at the seven-day chart; they look at how much inference capacity a token returns. It is a different way of being an asset, and it demands a different way of reading it.
