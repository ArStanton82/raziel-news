On the evening of 23 November Erik Voorhees wrote on X: «Venice just crossed $200m annualized revenue», citing his own post of 17 August, the one about the 100 million. Fourteen weeks to double, in a company that says it has been profitable since the first quarter of 2026. It is one of the fastest growth stories in consumer AI. It is also, for now, a sentence.

## What that number means

Annualised revenue is not a year's turnover: it is a recent period projected over twelve months. If the pace held, it would be around 16.7 million a month. Venice has not said which period it annualised, nor how it breaks down between subscriptions, API calls and purchased credits. Public pricing runs from 18 to 200 dollars a month, plus credits (100 = 1 dollar), and in July the company spoke of 3.5 million registered users and 1.3 trillion tokens a month: it has never said how many of them pay. Those who have tried to reconstruct the figure — The AI Receipts, which checked the data on 24 November — label it explicitly «reported, not verified».

## The only verifiable shadow

DefiLlama counts as fees, by its declared methodology, the dollar value of VVV bought back and burned: 1.05 million in November, 1.06 in October, 5.02 cumulative. Projected over twelve months, the burn would be worth about 12.6 million, 6.3% of the claimed run rate; over the current month alone, 0.5%. It is not a rebuttal: buyback is a policy, not a levy on revenue, and Venice settles subscriptions, API and DIEM off-chain. But it is the only quantity that can be checked without trusting a post, and it says that the link between revenue and the token is indirect. TVL, again on DefiLlama, stays at zero; staking is a separate series at 849 million dollars, 69.65% of the market cap.

## The market buys the narrative

VVV is worth 24.7 dollars according to CoinGecko and 24.80 according to DefiLlama, with +17.9% over seven days converging across the two sources, while bitcoin loses 9.2% over the same interval. Market cap 1.21 billion, fully diluted valuation 2.0. The token remains 28.5% below the 34.53 dollar high of 21 September: no price «all-time high», whatever the posts may suggest.

The useful question is not whether Venice is making 200 million, but how much of that revenue is recurring and how much goes through the token. As long as the answer sits in an announcement, the number has to be quoted as a statement — respectable, verifiable tomorrow, not yet a fact.
