There is an honest way and a dishonest way to tell the Venice story at the end of this October. The honest one starts from a small, verifiable fact: over the last thirty days the platform has burned more than a million dollars' worth of VVV. The dishonest one sets a big number beside it — one and a half billion dollars of value locked — that does not exist on any of the pages cited. The difference between a protocol that grows and a story that grows shows up in the decimals.

## The burner, measured

DefiLlama records $1,031,354 in fees for Venice over the past thirty days, $22,475 in the last twenty-four hours and $3.93 million cumulative. VeniceStats, an independent tracker, breaks the figure down: the discretionary cycle of 7 October burned 16,124 VVV at $25.72, that is $414,710, through a TWAP funded in USDC; the rest comes from the programmatic burns tied to subscriptions — $2 for Pro, $5 for Pro+, $10 for Max — and from credit purchases, about $3,100 a day.

The two sources do not copy each other and they converge, including on the trajectory: three months ago the burner consumed about $9,000 a day, yesterday almost $24,000. Calling the whole monthly amount a "voluntary buyback" is wrong twice over, because the largest part is by now automatic and indexed to the subscription. One limit has to be stated: DefiLlama excludes subscriptions, API and DIEM, which settle off-chain. The burn is the on-chain shadow of revenue, not revenue.

## The number that isn't there

Beside the million, the report of the day carried a "Venice TVL" of $1.56 billion. On the DefiLlama page TVL is zero, and not by mistake: the methodology counts only tokens locked in liquidity contracts, which Venice does not have. Staking is kept as a separate series: $887 million, 71.75 percent of circulating market cap. VeniceStats confirms from the other side, 33.56 million VVV in staking, 66.61 percent of the circulating supply.

That billion and a half is not a harmless slip: it is staking added to a market cap, presented as a third quantity. The result is a protocol that looks bigger than it is.

## The number that is missing

At the October call, relayed by Bankless, Venice was said to have passed five million users and a trillion tokens a day. The first figure has a single source, the description of an event, and it clashes with the site's About page, which still speaks of "more than one million users"; the second has to be weighed against what Venice claimed in July: 3.5 million users and 1.3 trillion tokens a month. A trillion a day would be twenty-three times as much in four months. Possible, perhaps. Verified, no.

A million burned in a month is a real story, and already an uncomfortable one for those selling the token. The other two items, for now, remain narrative: we do not publish them. A newspaper's job is not to complete the picture with the numbers that are missing, but to say which numbers are there.
