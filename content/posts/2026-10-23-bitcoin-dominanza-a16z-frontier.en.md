22 October reads from a single indicator: Bitcoin's share of total crypto market value hit 60%, the highest level since May. This is not a rally. While dominance rises, BTC drops below 82,000 dollars after grazing 84,700: a selective retreat, and it describes a market that is narrowing rather than widening.

## The refuge inside the refuge

US spot ETFs took in 528 million on Wednesday, with 23 million on Tuesday and 526.6 on Monday: 1.08 billion over three sessions, according to SoSoValue. October turns positive by roughly 461 million; BlackRock leads with 235 million, Fidelity follows at 213. But the figure lands after two weeks of outflows, 228 and 681 million: flows follow price more than they anticipate it, and that money is not buying altcoins.

## The name that is no longer needed

On the same day a16z gave up the word that made it famous. Chris Dixon announced that a16z Crypto becomes a16z Frontier, widening the mandate from crypto startups to "frontier startups": artificial intelligence, robotics, markets that take years. Five months ago the 2.2 billion fund had been presented as "100% dedicated to crypto entrepreneurs". Dixon, who leads the structure with Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz, argues that "the distinction between crypto companies and other companies is becoming less useful". It is not an abandonment: it is a change of label while capital moves.

## The merger that isn't

On infrastructure, Fireblocks denied Axios: no advanced talks to be bought by Visa for around 2.5 billion. "This is news to us, and it is not true at this time," the company told The Block; Visa did not comment. Fireblocks was worth 8 billion at its last public valuation, in January 2022. The rumour remains an indicator: Visa has passed 20 billion in annualised stablecoin settlement volume and Mastercard has already bought BVNK. Whoever guards payments wants custody.

## Rules and borders

The Blockchain Association wrote to the four leaders of the House and Senate asking for tax reform: an exemption for fees under ten dollars, privacy, loss-selling rules extended to crypto. The Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act passed the Ways and Means committee in September but will not resume before the November elections; the Clarity Act stalled at the first procedural vote in the Senate.

The rest is a question of jurisdiction. Chinese prosecutors want to confiscate more than 61,800 bitcoin tied to a 5.7 billion fraud, with 102,000 savers involved: the Tianjin court has opened six months for claims, but the bitcoin was seized in Britain, and London's High Court ruled on 12 October that Chinese law governs the victims' claims.

The picture is less exciting and more solid than the price: capital concentrates in Bitcoin, venture firms change their names, infrastructure gets bought. The speculative part is shrinking, the institutional part is not.
