Sunday morning Bitcoin is trading below 76 thousand dollars, after touching a seven-week low on Thursday. That is the price you see. The figure that explains the week, though, sits in a different count: the twelve U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs lost 1.28 billion dollars in net outflows across the five sessions to 6 November, four of them closing in the red.

## One week erases nine months

The sequence is that of an orderly and fast withdrawal: 398.6 million out on Monday, 115.6 on Tuesday, a single positive session on Wednesday with 74.6 million coming in, then 430.2 on Thursday and 411.9 on Friday. The weekly total is the worst since late June — when 1.79 billion had left — and it is the second negative week in a row; on the list of worst weeks since the funds launched in January 2024 it sits seventh.

The real blow lands on the yearly balance. The funds went into this week with roughly 368 million in net inflows for 2026, after turning positive in late September; they came out of it with about 914 million in outflows since the start of the year. A cushion built since late September vanished in five days, and it says one simple thing: it had never been big enough to absorb a shock. On Friday BlackRock's IBIT alone weighed in at 403 million, 88% of the day's gross outflows, while Fidelity's FBTC took in 42.8 million: even inside the same segment, the movement is concentrated. Ether ETFs lost another 365.3 million, their sixth negative week in a row.

## The context changes sign

In the same week American voters handed Democrats control of both chambers. For the crypto industry that means closer scrutiny of its dealings with the White House and, above all, the end of the legislative window: the CLARITY Act, passed by the House 294 to 134 in July 2025, stalled in the Senate on 15 September with 49 votes against 50 on a procedural motion that required 60. The sitting Congress closes on 3 January 2027, and the bill in its current form closes with it. What is left is the lame-duck session, with the same arithmetic as September and an interlocutor — an incoming Democratic Senate — with no hurry to concede anything.

## Two convictions withdrawn together

It is worth resisting the temptation to read the ETF decline as a verdict on bitcoin. A fund that loses 411 million in a day does not change its mind about digital gold: it records that its client has decided to give it less room. The withdrawal matches the withdrawal of regulatory expectations, and the coincidence is instructive: 2026's institutional demand had been built on a thesis — clear rules, broad adoption — that the calendar refuted before the market did. Outflows do not measure the end of an idea. They measure how little margin had been set aside for the case in which the idea was late.
