September is statistically Bitcoin's worst month: an average loss of about 2.9% over thirteen years. September 2026 shut that door with a gain of around 10%, the best since 2012. It is not a superstition falling away: it is the moment the market stops behaving like a niche bet and starts behaving like infrastructure.

## A month against the historical average

End-of-month estimates place the advance between 10% and 11%, with a cycle high of $86,979 on 21 September; mid-month, other counts stopped at a more modest +3%. A timid first half, an explosive second one, with the break of the 365-day moving average after 310 days without it. Institutions, meanwhile, have stopped selling: Bitwise's report on fifteen large investors — pension funds, endowments, foundations, sovereign funds — shows that none reduced exposure during the 50% drawdown between late 2025 and spring 2026, and several bought. For all of them Bitcoin is the first and largest asset; price was never cited as a reason to exit.

## The treasuries don't stop

BitMine (BMNR) has passed 6 million ETH: 6,001,302 tokens as of 27 September, about 4.9% of supply, for a value of roughly $16.2 billion. Strive (ASST) added 1,107 Bitcoin between 21 and 25 September at an average price of $85,396, reaching 27,462 BTC and +265% year to date. Two different ways of using the market as a vault: accumulating the asset you consider money, or the one you consider collateral.

## Traditional finance enters the building site

On the infrastructure side, the week tells the same transition. MoonPay launched its Korean subsidiary with Woori Bank, KB Financial Group and KakaoBank, on remittances and stablecoin settlement. Jeeves raised $110 million in a Series C led by CoinFund for stablecoin wallets and instant payouts in 190 countries: more than half of international volumes already settle in stablecoin. Kalshi trades a billion at a valuation of around $40 billion, almost double May's. And Cboe, renewing its exclusive licence with S&P Dow Jones for twenty-five years, wrote into the note a hypothesis worth more than the extension itself: tokenised options.

## Who stays outside the door

Not everything moves in the same direction. The CLARITY Act — the text that would have split oversight between the SEC and the CFTC — failed its procedural vote 49-50 on 15 September: in today's report it turns up as fresh news, but it is a re-emergence. In El Salvador the government called Bloomberg's report on the Sivar app, built with Modveon on Coinbase infrastructure, "fake news": after selling Chivo, it says it has no further involvement in crypto or stablecoin wallets.

The direction is clear, the consensus is not. The market has started climbing again, but the regulatory frame that should consolidate that rise is still under construction.
