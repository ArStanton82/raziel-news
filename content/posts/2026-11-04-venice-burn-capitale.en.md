There is a sentence Venice has repeated for months and put in writing on 3 November: "Venice's capital asset is VVV. It is your key to the Port City of AI." Behind the metaphor sits a precise mechanism — staking, DIEM minting, daily credit — and next to the mechanism sits a number worth looking at without enthusiasm: how much of VVV has already been destroyed.

## A number that changes sign with the base

Venice's official burn tracker reports 33.8 million VVV burned, 42% of supply; 29% shows as staking, 10.7% as locked. The "over 40%" figure circulated by @AskVenice is therefore correct, but it is not the only possible reading: against a larger original supply the same quantity weighs less. This is the kind of detail that separates a datum from a slogan, and one that is always worth checking on the tracker instead of copying.

On the liquidity side, DefiLlama still shows a TVL of zero dollars: by the aggregator's own methodology the protocol holds no value locked in the strict sense, and staking is kept as a separate series, roughly 803 million dollars, 70.9% of market capitalisation. Anyone who tells you about a TVL of one and a half billion is adding together two things the aggregator keeps apart.

## A million a month, but not revenue

The most solid reading on burns comes from DefiLlama's "Fees" series, which by the aggregator's own declaration measures the dollar value of the VVV bought back and burned: 1.06 million in October, 833 thousand in September, 697 thousand in August, 4.07 million cumulative. The same methodology warns, however, that subscriptions, API access and DIEM credits are excluded because they settle off-chain.

This is the distinction that matters. The burn is the on-chain shadow of revenue, not revenue itself: it grows when subscriptions grow, but it does not say how much Venice actually takes in. On the monthly call with Bankless, strategy lead Jon estimated that 70-80% of activity is paid and that burns have grown 16-20 times since December; those are statements made in a public conversation, not a set of accounts.

## Four million or five?

On the same call David Hoffman said Venice has passed 5 million users, taking 68 days to go from four to five million, and showed a chart where daily token volume rose from about 250 billion to over a thousand billion in October. Unfortunately Venice's About page still reads "over 4 million users": two sources from the same company that do not line up.

It is not a scandalous contradiction, it is a communications misalignment, but that is the point. When a growth metric appears only in a call or a post, it has to be cited as a claim. The product, meanwhile, keeps moving: VVV trades between 23 and 24 dollars and the market cap stays just above one billion. The editorial question remains the same: is Venice becoming infrastructure, or a good story about a token that gets consumed?
