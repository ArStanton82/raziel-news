The number to start from is not today's price, it is Sunday's. In Asian trading Bitcoin fell to 62,112 dollars, the lowest since July 14: about 25 per cent of its value gone since October 21. Then the rebound, first to 64,843 dollars and this morning around 64,700, up 1 per cent over 24 hours with a range that has stayed between 63,900 and 66,000 in recent hours.

A four-month low is not news in itself. It becomes news when you look at what came before it.

## The technical break

The slide accelerated after the loss of the 200-day moving average, around 73,000 dollars. From there two movements started feeding each other: fresh short positions and the forced closing of longs. During the week Bitcoin also broke below the 200-week average, which on November 14 ran around 67,992 dollars — a threshold that in market language counts as the border between a bull cycle and a deep correction.

## Liquidations and flows

Coinglass counts about 2 billion dollars of positions liquidated in the 24 hours to Sunday morning in Singapore: 774 million on Bitcoin, 488 on Ethereum, mostly long. Glassnode flags a cluster of potential liquidations between 59,500 and 62,600 dollars, just below the lows actually touched: that is why Monday's rebound does not settle the matter.

On the institutional side the signal is slower and heavier. US spot ETFs recorded almost 3 billion dollars of net outflows between October 30 and November 13, with roughly 1.07 billion in the last week and seven consecutive sessions of redemption; over two weeks the balance is 2.35 billion. Stablecoin supply contracted by 17 billion in a month, meaning liquidity left the system.

## The noise beside the fact

In the same hours two statements circulated that should not be treated as news: Michael Saylor's hint at an imminent announcement and the line from Strive's CEO that "Bitcoin is cheap, we want more of it". Neither comes with a filing or a quantity. They remain hypotheses, and as such should be left.

What is worth reading is the move for what it is: a correction driven by leverage in a market with less marginal liquidity. Sunday's price is a fact verified three times over; its explanation is still a hypothesis. Of the two, only the first belongs on the front page.
