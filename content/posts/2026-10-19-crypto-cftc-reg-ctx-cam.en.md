For years crypto regulation in the United States came out of courtrooms: one enforcement action at a time, with interpretation as the battleground. The document published on 13 October in the Federal Register tries to move the ground. It is not a rule, and that needs saying straight away: it is an advance notice of proposed rulemaking, which is to say a long, structured question. But it is the first time the Commodity Futures Trading Commission has put on paper the architecture of a national regime for retail crypto-asset trading.

## Two rulebooks, one fork in the road

The ANPRM, adopted on 5 October and published on the 13th (91 FR 64811, RIN 3038-AF80, 17 CFR parts 1, 38 and 39), turns on two structures. The first, Reg CTX, clarifies which crypto-asset transactions fall under section 2(c)(2)(D) of the Commodity Exchange Act: the perimeter of the so-called CTX, retail transactions that are leveraged, margined or financed. The second, Reg CAM, builds the place where those transactions may happen. Comments must be received by 14 December 2026.

## A designated market, made to measure

Reg CAM introduces a subcategory of designated contract market reserved for venues trading exclusively in CTX, christened "crypto asset market". The idea, set out also in the analyses by Sidley and STB Law, is to adapt the DCM Core Principles to the reality of crypto rather than applying the futures framework unfiltered. Transactions would pass through a futures commission merchant and clearing through a derivatives clearing organisation, with calibrated requirements. Joining would be voluntary: an exchange can stay outside and keep operating as it does today, but without the badge of federal supervision. In comments the CFTC relayed at the Fordham Law Blockchain Regulatory Symposium, the goal is described as a uniform path, offered as an alternative to the previous administration's regulation-by-enforcement.

## The market, meanwhile, is looking at China

Monday's price reaction says nothing about rules. Bitcoin trades around 82,500 dollars, up 1.3% over 24 hours, with a daily range between 80,671 and 83,035. What moves it is the macro: Chinese third-quarter GDP grew 4.5% year on year, above the 4.3% of the previous quarter, and retail sales rose 1.1% against 0.5% expected. Nasdaq futures are up more than 1%, the Nikkei 1.8%, the Kospi 0.6%; oil falls on expectations of greater supply tied to the Russian waiver, gold holds around 4,180 dollars and the dollar sits below 102. Crypto and tech stocks move together, as increasingly happens.

The date to mark is not the headline's, it is 14 December: that is when it becomes clear whether the manual will have any pages written. In the meantime the asymmetry is worth noting. The most solid part of the day is a document of questions; the noisiest is a 1.3% gain that depends on Chinese GDP. A market that says it is waiting for the rules, and then moves on something else, is a market that has already decided what to watch.
