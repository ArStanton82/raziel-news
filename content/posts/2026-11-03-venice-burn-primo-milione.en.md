There is a difference between a token that promises to shrink supply and one that does it with verifiable regularity. Venice belongs to the second category, and October is the month the tally changed order of magnitude: according to DefiLlama, the dollar value of the VVV bought back and burned reached 1.06 million, the first month above one million since the series exists.

## A curve that never bent

The monthly progression is almost monotonic: 64,402 dollars in December 2025, 240,206 in June, 447,576 in July, 697,337 in August, 833,084 in September. No month in decline. November, in its first three days, already shows 68,329 dollars.

The figure is solid because the methodology is declared: for DefiLlama, Venice's "Fees" line is exactly the dollar value of the VVV bought on the market and burned, and it coincides with the "Holders Revenue" line. The cumulative total, 4.04 million, matches almost to the cent the 4.15 million of "all-time spent" that the independent tracker VeniceStats records on the protocol's account.

## The on-chain shadow of revenue

Here, though, begins the part the number alone does not tell. The same methodology warns that subscriptions, API and credit purchases in DIEM settle off-chain, by card, Coinbase Commerce and Stripe: they leave no on-chain trace and do not enter the series. The burn is therefore the shadow of revenue, not revenue itself.

It helps to read the composition. VeniceStats separates three flows: the discretionary monthly buyback, a USDC-funded TWAP that on 7 October burned 16,124 VVV at 25.72 dollars, for 414,710 dollars; the programmatic burns per subscription (2 dollars for Pro, 5 for Pro+, 10 for Max); and those triggered by credit purchases. The next discretionary cycle is expected on 8 November, with an estimated range between 352 and 678 thousand dollars.

## The TVL that is not there

None of these numbers, however, licenses talk of deposited liquidity. On the DefiLlama page Venice's TVL is zero, and not by mistake: the methodology counts the VVV in staking, but the page keeps staking as a separate series and warns that it is not part of TVL. Staking is worth 726.88 million dollars, 70.42% of a market capitalisation just above one billion.

Adding the two lines together, as circulates in some reconstructions, produces a figure that exists in no source. The same goes for price: among aggregators the gap on VVV is a few percentage points, enough to rule out any 24-hour change. Better to stick to the order of magnitude.

The lesson is sober: a recurring, verifiable burn is a fact; its interpretation as structural demand is a hypothesis that requires reading also what stays off-chain.
