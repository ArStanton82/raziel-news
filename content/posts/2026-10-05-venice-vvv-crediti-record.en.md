Venice's token keeps running and, for once, the price is not the only interesting figure. Alongside the quote, the usage numbers emerge: credits consumed, sign-ups, revenue burned. They are the part that tells you whether the narrative holds.

## The W40 report

The weekly W40 report from the analysis account @venicestats — a third-party, unofficial observer — paints a picture of growth: VVV at 29.93 dollars on a weekly basis (+2.7 per cent), AskVenice credit consumption at a channel high of about 2,601 per day, sign-ups up 20.1 per cent to 17,487, the sixth consecutive week of gains. These are figures declared by an outside analyst and should be read as such: no confirmation comes from Venice, whose official blog has published nothing since 29 September.

## The market numbers

On the market VVV hovers between 29.4 and 29.7 dollars. CoinGecko quotes it at 29.71 (+7 per cent in 24 hours), DefiLlama at 29.50-29.54, CoinStats at 29.38 (+5.99 per cent). The market capitalisation is about 1.42-1.44 billion, daily volume between 30 and 33 million. The all-time high is 34.53 dollars, reached on 21 September 2026: the token is roughly 14 per cent below that level, but is up 4.4 per cent over seven days and 62.6 per cent over thirty.

## Where the value lies

VVV is not a governance token: it is staked for yield, and locked staking (sVVV) mints DIEM, Venice's API credit. Every staked DIEM is worth one dollar a day of inference credit; the yield is 80 per cent to the staker and 20 per cent to Venice, with a seven-day wait to unlock. Since 1 October annual emissions have fallen to 2 million VVV. DefiLlama counts only the on-chain buy-and-burn as revenue: about 842,000 dollars over the last thirty days, 3.66 million on an annual basis. Everything else — subscriptions, API, credits — settles off-chain and leaves no trace on the blockchain.

## Conclusion

A token that rises 62 per cent in a month tells you about expectation, not use. The part that matters is the other one: working out whether credit consumption and sign-ups will keep growing once the enthusiasm has cooled, and whether the buy-and-burn will manage to outpace new emissions.
