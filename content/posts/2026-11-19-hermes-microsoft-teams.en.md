The open-source agent spent years living where its users live: a terminal, a configuration file, a virtual machine. On 18 November Nous Research announced that Hermes is arriving where its new users are, the ones who want nothing to do with terminals: Microsoft Teams, the Microsoft Store, Azure Container Apps sandboxes. Read as a product announcement, it is a list of integrations. Read for what it is, it marks the passage of an agent born for developers into the perimeter of large organisations.

## The place where work happens

The details are dry and concrete. The Windows desktop app arrives on Microsoft's store. In Teams you assign real tasks — financial analysis, document drafts, inbox triage — and you read the result in the same conversation where the request started. The agent's workloads run in isolated microVMs that, according to the Microsoft blog of 17 November, boot in under a second and release compute when idle. Anyone with data-residency constraints can install Hermes Enterprise in their own Azure tenant.

That Nous is treated as an ecosystem vendor is no bolt from the blue: the announcement itself calls it an «M12 portfolio company», Microsoft's fund, and Nous's note on the round closed on 7 October lists M12 among the investors in the $90 million Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation. The partnership is the continuation of a relationship already written into the capital.

## The real merchandise is habits

The feature worth attention is not the integration, it is Collective Wisdom: skills — the written definition of a method plus the code that executes its stable steps — can be proposed and published for the team, with review. Nous says they remain a private asset of the organisation and do not become training data for a vendor.

This is where the centre of gravity shifts. If the model counts less and less and the internal library of «how this particular job is done» counts more and more, the cost of switching vendor stops sitting in the model and starts sitting in the processes the company has deposited inside the agent. The flip side: how solid the review of shared skills is cannot be verified from the outside, and the enterprise plans on the Marketplace appear as private offers, without a price list.

## The figures are the company's own

The announcement calls Hermes «number one among public open agents on OpenRouter, with almost 2 trillion tokens a day». Nous also estimates that the agent accounts for about 2.5% of the AI tokens consumed worldwide. These are claims, not independent measurements: they should be cited as such, and the order of magnitude kept for what it is.

Next quarter's question is not whether Hermes will be inside Teams — it already is — but whether shared skills will actually be reused, or will stay an elegant archive nobody opens. The integration is a fact. Adoption is still a hypothesis.
