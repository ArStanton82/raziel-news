There are weeks when price and infrastructure tell the same story. The one that closed on Sunday 27 September is one of them: a market climbing back and, beneath the surface, a sector rewriting its own rules on security and access.

## The close that had been missing for eight months

Bitcoin closed the week at $84,450 — $84,467 on another reading — the highest weekly close since late January 2026, about 8% above the previous week and more than 40% above the June lows. The intraday peak had come on Monday 21, at $87,397, with $715 million of daily inflows into the ETFs and a billion dollars of forced short liquidations. Then the slide: by Monday 28 the price was already back below $83,000. A real rebound, but still with fragile legs.

## Goldman's Treasury fund arrives — not tokenised

Goldman Sachs is bringing FTIXX, its roughly $100 billion Treasury money market fund, onto Lynq, a settlement network for digital businesses built on a permissioned Avalanche L1, with tZERO Securities as a broker-dealer registered with the SEC. It is the first external asset manager on the network. The detail that deserves attention is a negative one: unlike BlackRock's BUIDL or Franklin Templeton's BENJI, FTIXX is not tokenised. Lynq is a distribution channel, not an on-chain representation. Access is reserved for US clients with a pre-existing relationship. Wall Street comes in, but keeps the door ajar.

## CCIP 2.0: the lesson of Kelp DAO

On 28 September Chainlink launched CCIP 2.0, which lets you overlay your own security verifiers onto cross-chain transfers. The move responds directly to the single-verifier vulnerability blamed for the $292 million hack of the Kelp DAO bridge (18 April, 116,500 rsETH, attributed to the Lazarus group). There is, however, a detail that circulates less: the Risk Management Network, the second set of nodes that re-checked transactions, no longer performs that role. Independent checking can now come from optional verifiers — and anyone who adds none is relying on a single verifier network, where there used to be two.

## Other signals

BitMNR passes 6 million ETH (6,001,302, about $16.1 billion at $2,698 per ETH) and is aiming for 5% of supply. Polymarket opens 15-minute Up/Down markets on Bitcoin on its US app, in 48 states plus the District of Columbia. Belarus registers the first two "crypto banks" in its High-Tech Park, after presidential decree No. 19 in January. Vitalik Buterin, meanwhile, called lean consensus "amazing" and outlined the shift to a "cryptographic world computer".

## Conclusion

The rally is real, but the most interesting part of the week lies elsewhere: in the architectures that decide who may enter and on what terms. Goldman chooses distribution without tokenisation; Chainlink shifts responsibility onto the client; Minsk designs a new licence. The market climbs again, the infrastructure goes back to arguing about itself.
