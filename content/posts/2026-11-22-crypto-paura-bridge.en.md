A frightened market is not a prudent market. That is the lesson of this weekend: Bitcoin fell below 60,000 dollars for the first time since July and, while fear touched its low for the year, two protocols lost funds within hours.

## Fear has a number, and not just one

On Friday Bitcoin slid to 58,811 dollars, closing around 59,500 and trading near 59,000 in the following hours: roughly 5.6 percent lower in 24 hours, 13.8 percent on the week. It is not isolated: the Nasdaq lost 5.28 percent, the S&P 500 3.69 percent, Anthropic 8.5 percent on its debut. When the valuation of artificial intelligence is called into question, risk gets sold everywhere.

The Fear & Greed index sits at «extreme fear»: readings oscillate between 9 and 12, but sentiment had not been this depressed since February.

## Two thefts, two models of failure

On Saturday Chainflip confirmed the theft of about 12 million dollars from its vaults: 122.86 Bitcoin, 520.22 Ether, 3.25 million USDC and 758,141 USDT. Twice the initial estimate of 4.86 million, which covered only the Ethereum vault. The protocol offered the attacker 10 percent to return 90 percent by 18:38 UTC on 23 November, promising to treat it as a white-hat recovery and to withdraw its complaints. The groundwork dates back to 11 November through Tornado Cash, with a validator account registered on Friday: the signature of a planned attack. FLIP lost more than 40 percent in a day.

Hours earlier another bridge had given way. Cyber detected an «unexpected minting» of 5.21 million tokens on Solana, followed two minutes later by the release of the same amount from the Ethereum lockbox. The tokens were sold for about 968,000 dollars in ETH; Upbit and Bithumb suspended deposits and withdrawals. Two incidents in one day are not a trend, but they indicate where the risk concentrates: the authority to mint, not the cryptography.

## Monday's court

On Friday federal judge Lorna Schofield rejected the CFTC's second emergency request to block the New York attorney general's lawsuit against Kalshi. On Monday at 11:30 ET judge Melissa Crane will rule on the restraining order: barring Kalshi from operating «within or from New York». The company, headquartered in New York, argues that the order would shut it down across the whole country; the attorney general's office denies that this is the aim. Meanwhile Casten and Lieu propose paying whistleblowers 10 to 30 percent of sanctions on prediction markets, above one million dollars: it is not law, but it says that lawmakers have stopped treating event markets as an experiment.

## The common thread

Price, security, rules: three surfaces of the same problem. A sector that keeps losing funds to a compromised key and arguing in court over whether a contract is gambling. The fear of these days is not irrational: it is the price of an infrastructure that grows faster than its guarantees.
