This morning bitcoin is worth between 58,900 and 59,200 dollars according to CoinGecko and Cointelegraph: just under 9.2% in a week, over 26% in a month. The level describes the move better than any twenty-four-hour percentage, which shifts from hour to hour. And around the price sit two stories that are not about the price: one about flows, the other about security.

## The number: 4.94 billion

According to data gathered by Bloomberg up to 24 November, 4.94 billion dollars net have left the American spot funds since the first of the month: it would be the worst month of outflows since the products debuted in January 2024, beyond the previous record of 4.51 billion in June. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust alone has lost over 4 billion — 4.17 according to Yahoo Finance — with a single session of inflows in the whole of November and six consecutive days of selling, the last for 231.56 million. It is worth spelling out which figure is being used: Bloomberg itself reported 4.71 billion as of 23 November; the difference is one session, but it is the difference between two numbers that are both in circulation.

## Why it happened

21Shares, in a report dated 24 November, separates the causes. The 6.4 billion hack suffered by Binance on 29 October set it off, but bitcoin lost only 5.1% in the two following days and recovered almost all of it by 4 November. Then weak demand took away another 8.3% between 4 and 11 November, and forced liquidations the rest: down 13.5% in three days, 5.8 billion of positions closed in the week to 15 November, 81% of them long. Stablecoin supply contracted by 10.7 billion, the widest eighteen-day decline since 2022. The average realized price, 53,250 dollars, is the level the same company points to as the threshold to watch.

## The second crack

On 23 November Coinkite published the postmortem of the Coldcard flaw: at least fifteen independent security reviews, including those done with AI, failed to notice that the seed generator used a weaker system than the one the tests were checking. The result is at least 1,789 bitcoin stolen, 114.7 million dollars at the time of the thefts according to Galaxy Research. The flaw was fixed on 31 July; by 18 November the company had received around 300 reports of losses tied to 242 accounts, believes the victims are more numerous, and has not yet settled on a compensation plan. A software update does not make seeds already exposed safe, and that has to be said to anyone who owns a device.

Two different stories, same object. ETFs sell when security cracks; a hardware wallet is as safe as the process that verifies it, not as safe as the claim on the box. The market is not pricing only bitcoin: it is pricing the possibility of keeping it.
