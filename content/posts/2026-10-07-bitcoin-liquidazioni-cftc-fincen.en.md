Two stories are running in parallel right now. One is the price, which is falling. The other is American regulation, which is accelerating. In between, a market that has learned to live with both.

## Oil sets the pace

Overnight Bitcoin fell below 84,000 dollars, touching about 83,840 dollars during the Asian session, down roughly 1.5%. The cause is not crypto: Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz pushed Brent above 100 dollars a barrel, strengthening the dollar and Treasury yields. The result was a cascade of liquidations: according to CoinGlass, about 485 million dollars of long positions were closed, out of total liquidations of around 550 million. The level to watch, according to FxPro, is 83,000 dollars: below that threshold the road to 80,000 would open quickly.

## The CFTC writes the rules on its own

On the regulatory front, on 5 October the CFTC opened a public consultation on two frameworks — Regulation CTX and Regulation CAM — for retail transactions in crypto commodities, with a registration category dedicated to exchanges offering leverage, margin or financing. A caveat: it is an "advance notice of proposed rulemaking", the first step in the process, not a rule. Public comment stays open for 60 days from publication in the Federal Register. Chairman Michael Selig, on CNBC, did not hide his frustration at the CLARITY Act's blockage in the Senate: "I am absolutely disappointed in Congress... but we have statutory authority and we will use it".

## FinCEN withdraws two proposals

The Treasury moves in the opposite direction. FinCEN has formally withdrawn two proposals that never entered into force: the 2020 one on self-hosted wallets, which would have required collecting information above 3,000 dollars and reporting above 10,000, and the 2023 one on crypto mixers. It is not a repeal of existing obligations — those rules never became final — but the closing of two files that had weighed on self-custody for years.

## The rest of the picture

Around these two stories everything else is moving fast: Pudgy Penguins' Abstract L2 will shut down on 15 December after losses in the tens of millions (the second L2 to close in a week, after Blast); Winklevoss Asset Services has filed an S-1 for a spot Zcash ETF; FNB in South Africa has opened Bitcoin purchases to nearly nine million customers; Cardano has activated CIP-0113, the standard that lets issuers freeze and seize assets. The common thread is clear: while the price trembles, infrastructure and rules are settling into place.
