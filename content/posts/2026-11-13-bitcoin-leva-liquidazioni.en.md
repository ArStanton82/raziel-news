At three in the morning Italian time Bitcoin was still around 70,800 dollars. Ninety minutes later it was below 69,000, and over the following four hours about 800 million dollars of positions ceased to exist. It is the kind of move you understand by looking at where the money was sitting before the cause arrived.

## The number, before the reasons

The drop is sharp: about 7% over 24 hours, with the price between 68,400 and 68,700 dollars according to The Block and CoinGecko (which this morning showed 68,666, -6.5%). It is the lowest level since August 20; Ether fell below 1,930 dollars.

The liquidations tell the mechanics better than the price does. CoinGlass, cited by The Block at 00:10 ET, counted 1.47 billion dollars liquidated in 24 hours across 175,299 traders, of which 1.31 billion in long positions. The same source, read by The Crypto Times at 04:46 UTC, reported 1.41 billion across 172,922 traders, with 89% longs. The two figures do not contradict each other: they are two frames of a rolling window that empties as you watch. What counts is the proportion: nine positions out of ten were bets on the way up.

## Two triggers and a pre-existing fragility

There are two immediate causes, both outside the crypto perimeter. The first is the earthquake at the top of Nvidia: on November 12 the board announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as chairman and chief executive with immediate effect, after the board's inquiry into extramarital relationships that had emerged in the preceding weeks. Colette Kress, chief financial officer, runs the company in the interim. The company communicated it in an investor relations notice, and the general press followed.

The second is the Fed, but the opposite way round from how we are used to writing it: the market is pricing a new rate hike, not a cut, and debating whether it might be 50 basis points instead of 25. In that setting the liquidity that seeks risk withdraws, and the most leveraged assets empty out first.

Neither piece of news is about Bitcoin. Both found the same vulnerability: 45.3 billion dollars of open interest on perpetuals, with long concentrations just above round numbers. Whoever bought on margin below 72,000 dollars did not have a wrong thesis about the protocol; they had a position that could not survive a risk-off Tuesday.

## What is left after the flush

The figure to watch is not the price but the funding: if the cost of holding a long position stays high, leverage rebuilds and the next move will carry the same multiplier. If it flattens, the market is digesting the loss rather than reopening it.

There is one detail that shrinks the comfortable reading of "mere technical panic": US spot ETFs recorded a fifth consecutive session of outflows. The institutional demand that for months had absorbed supply is behaving like the rest of the market. A crash nobody predicted is still a crash; a crash that finds the buyers already retreating is a longer story.
