Venice has chosen to put privacy front and centre just as the token retreats. On 12 October the official @AskVenice account published a thread on confidentiality: conversations live locally, Venice does not store them and cannot see them. It is not a technical novelty, but a positioning statement.

## Local chats, encrypted backups

The mechanism is described in the project's FAQs: history stays on the device, requests pass through the server but are not retained. Those who pay get access to the "TEE" and "E2EE" tiers, where the request is encrypted on the device and decrypted only inside a secure environment. For backups, the user encrypts the data with a password only they know, uploads it in fragments nobody else can open and restores it elsewhere. Venice does not keep that password: lose it and the backup is unrecoverable. It is an explicit trade-off between control and convenience.

## The staking exit queue, out in the open

The independent tracker VeniceStats has exposed the entire VVV staking exit queue: what is unlocking, what frees up day by day and what is already withdrawable. It added a "Daily flow" toggle that shows the net flow between new stakes and exit requests. These are transparency tools: they make visible a pressure that usually stays hidden inside aggregate data.

## A whale lightens up: 100,908 VVV to Coinbase

In the same hours an address with a very low purchase cost — about 3.77 dollars per VVV — deposited 100,908 tokens on Coinbase, worth close to 2.34 million dollars. According to on-chain analysts, the same wallet moved roughly 562,000 VVV to the exchange over four months, at an average price of about 21.26 dollars. A deposit to an exchange is not the same as a sale, but it signals an intention.

## Price and usage, again pulling in opposite directions

The day's price stays weak: VVV trades around 23 dollars, down about 7.7% over 24 hours, with the protocol's TVL on DefiLlama at roughly 1.52 billion dollars. Usage, however, is rising. VeniceStats' Weekly Report W41 records sign-ups up 6% to 18,529, the seventh consecutive weekly increase, and credit burns at record levels. Since 1 October VVV's annual emissions have dropped to 2 million tokens, while the programmatic burn of 5 dollars per 100 in credits remains in place.

## Conclusion

Venice is building two things in parallel: a product whose value proposition is data confidentiality, and a token whose promise is scarcity. The first advances through announcements and features; the second depends on on-chain flows, which today tell of large holders lightening up. They are two different clocks, and for now they do not show the same time.
