October closes with a record Venice had been chasing for months: for the first time the value of VVV burned in a single month tops one million dollars. That is little by traditional finance standards and a lot for a protocol that has burned less than four million in total so far.

## The counter

The on-chain data collected by DefiLlama traces an unambiguous curve: 64 thousand dollars in December 2025, 235 thousand in May, 447 thousand in July, 697 thousand in August, 833 thousand in September and 1.01 million dollars in October, with two calendar days still left to count. The cumulative figure stands at 3.93 million. The methodology is explicit: that number is the dollar value of VVV bought back and burned, meaning programme subscriptions plus the voluntary buybacks funded by revenue.

In the background sits the monetary side. Since 1 October annual emissions have dropped to 2 million tokens, roughly 86 per cent less than the 14 million at launch, after a ladder of cuts that began in May. Cumulative burns, airdrop included, exceed 33.7 million VVV. The stated goal is net deflation: more tokens out than in.

## How a burn is made

It is worth opening the mechanism, because the million is not a treasury cash splash. The monthly voluntary burn is a buyback spread over time: Venice funds a multisig in USDC and a trading protocol executes small purchases at close intervals, then around the 7th or 8th of the following month it burns the accumulated VVV. The 7 October cycle burned 16,124 VVV, worth 414,710 dollars at the price of the time.

The rest comes from programme burns: 2 dollars for every new Pro subscriber, 5 for Pro+, 10 for Max, plus 5 dollars for every 100 of credits purchased. It is a tax on growth, not a budget decision: if subscriptions slow down, that half of the counter slows down with them. The next cycle, scheduled for 8 November, is projected at between 316 and 478 thousand dollars.

## The price does not follow

Meanwhile the token trades just above 23 dollars, with a market capitalisation around 1.15 billion, roughly a third below the all-time high of 34.53 dollars touched on 21 September. Two thirds of the circulating supply is staked: 33.5 million VVV, worth more than 800 million dollars and accounting for about 71 per cent of the market cap.

On one point it is worth being precise, because an error keeps circulating: Venice's TVL is zero. Not out of weakness but out of methodology — DefiLlama keeps staking as a separate series, and the protocol's revenue is all there. Adding staking and market cap produces those billion-and-a-half figures that correspond to nothing measurable.

## The question that remains

A burn that grows while the price falls says two things at once. The first is that deflation alone does not make a price: 2 million tokens of annual emissions against a million dollars of monthly burn is a narrow gap, and the market is pricing something else, from future dilution to competition. The second is that the most solid part of Venice's business does not pass through the blockchain: subscriptions, API and credits settle off-chain, and DefiLlama excludes them by definition. The million dollars is the on-chain shadow of a larger revenue, not its measure.
