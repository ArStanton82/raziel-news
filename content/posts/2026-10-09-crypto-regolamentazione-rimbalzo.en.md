The crypto week moved along two tracks that rarely run together: geopolitics and regulation. On one side a price that breathes, on the other a wave of rules that thickens. Anyone watching only the chart risks missing the most interesting part.

## The rebound and geopolitics

Bitcoin returned to $82,000 on Friday, after falling to around 80,300. The push came from a statement: Donald Trump ruled out an attack on Iran before the midterm elections of 3 November, calling the talks "productive" while keeping the blockade in place. Oil pulled back, and fear with it. It is confirmation of how much the crypto market remains hostage to headlines rather than to its own fundamentals.

## The rules race: CFTC, ESMA, Greece

On the regulatory front, three tracks moved almost simultaneously. On 5 October the CFTC opened a preliminary consultation on a federal framework for retail crypto transactions, with two proposed rules — CTX and CAM — and a registration category dedicated to exchanges offering leverage, margin or financing; comments are expected within 60 days of publication in the Federal Register. On 8 October ESMA gave authorised platforms a three-month limit — until 8 January 2027 — to close their exposure to stablecoins that do not comply with MiCA, such as USDT. In Greece, finally, a bill introduces a 10% tax on crypto capital gains, with an exemption up to 500 euros a year and crypto-to-crypto swaps excluded from the calculation; the consultation closes on 22 October and the vote is expected in early November.

## Banks enter custody

While regulators tighten, traditional institutions widen. On 8 October Standard Chartered announced custody services for selected cryptocurrencies, stablecoins and tokenised real assets in Singapore, for institutional and accredited corporate clients. The same day, however, Democratic senator Richard Blumenthal pressed Cantor Fitzgerald over its ties to Tether, arguing that its 5% stake in the company has gone from 600 million to roughly 10 billion in estimated value: custody thus becomes a matter of national security too.

## A state bitcoin and cryptography under siege

On the technical side two stories still weigh. The US government moved 12,267 BTC — about 1.01 billion dollars — from a seizure wallet linked to the Bitfinex case, with no deposits to exchanges: a reorganisation, not a sale, but a reminder of the state's weight on the market. And Vitalik Buterin warned of the risks that AI-accelerated mathematics poses to cryptography, urging reduced exposure but without hasty migrations, which — he says — have cost him more than thefts.

## Conclusion

The price moves on geopolitical tweets; the infrastructure, by contrast, moves on consultations and deadlines. It is on the latter that the future of the sector is decided, not on a single day's rebound.
