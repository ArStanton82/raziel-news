Anyone watching only the price sees a market standing still: Bitcoin trades around 82,700 dollars, just below the intraday high of 83,124 touched on 20 October. Anyone watching the infrastructure sees three heavyweights moving in the same week.

## Switzerland opens, with a waiver

On 20 October FINMA approved the first Swiss retail fund investing directly in Bitcoin, classified as «other fund for alternative investments» with specific risk. To make it possible, the authority granted a waiver from the diversification rules, justified by Bitcoin's long trading history and by volumes far above those of other cryptos.

Two details matter. FINMA named neither the fund nor the issuer, and gave no launch date. And this is not an ETP — products of that kind have been listed in Switzerland for years — but a fund with fund status, subject to Swiss law: the custodian bank holds the Bitcoin and may delegate only to a custodian with equivalent supervision and bankruptcy protection.

## The 527 million that comes from a single fund

On 19 October US spot ETFs took in 526.7 million net, the best figure since 22 September, when it had been 714.7. But the number comes from a single fund: according to Farside, BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust gathered 665.2 million, while Fidelity lost 83.6 and Grayscale 54.9.

A record in one product is not broad demand: by the end of the day the ETFs were still negative by about 88 million since the start of October.

## Strive: 630 million, no mandate

Strive's SATS stock has landed on the Nasdaq: perpetual series A preferred shares with a 15 percent annual dividend, seven million shares at 90 dollars, for 630 million gross and about 608 net. The press release leaves the destination open: proceeds for «general corporate purposes, including the acquisition of bitcoin and related products». No commitment to convert all of it into Bitcoin.

Strive holds 36,818 BTC; at 82,500 dollars, the net proceeds would buy about 7,370. The full dividend is worth 105 million a year: more a debt raise than a bet on the upside.

## Paris taxes the exit into stablecoins

On 16 October the National Assembly adopted, by 38 votes to 37 with 25 abstentions, amendment I-3898 to the 2027 budget. From 1 January 2027 the conversion of crypto into MiCA-compliant e-money tokens becomes a taxable event, at a flat rate of 31.4 percent. USDT stays out, because it has no MiCA authorisation: the measure effectively hits USDC and EURC.

It is not law yet: the vote on the revenue section and the final vote are still missing, with the latter expected on 17 November. And the exit tax above 800,000 euros is not inside this amendment, but in a separate one still under discussion.

The thread linking these stories is not the rally. It is that the rules change shape before the prices do: a fund approved but unnamed, an inflow driven by a single product, a raise with no ring-fenced destination, a tax that shifts the moment at which a gain becomes taxable.
