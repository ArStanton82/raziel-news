Until yesterday an agent was something you configured. Since yesterday, on Venice, you put it in a shop window. The launch of Venice Minds and its Mind Market is the first time the platform tries to sell something other than access to a model.

## A catalogue of tasks, not of models

The official announcement is terse: build an AI for a single job, give it tools, publish it on the Mind Market, earn credits every time it is used. The creator keeps **90% of the margin**; the rest buys and burns VVV. You start from any paid plan.

The product page, already online, says how fast the take-off was: **3,024 active Minds**. And it makes the nature of the goods clear, which is not a generic chatbot but "the next generation of reasoning agents, characters and custom AI tools." A cinematographer who turns an idea into a shot list, a retoucher who doesn't touch what you didn't ask for, a fiction co-author. Narrow tasks, each with a name and an implicit price.

## The second burn engine

The economics were spelled out by head of strategy JonShapeShift: 100% of the platform fee on creator payouts goes into buying and burning VVV. It isn't an absolute first — the model already sends 5 dollars for every 100 in API credits to the burn — but it is a finer engine, one that follows usage instead of subscription.

The on-chain numbers remain small and legible. DefiLlama keeps a "fees" series for Venice that, by its declared methodology, is only the value of the VVV bought back and burned: 1.06 million dollars in October, 880,737 in November through the 18th, 4.85 million cumulative. The discretionary cycle of 9 November burned 21,657 VVV for 458,200 dollars, 10.9% more than the previous month, and the next one is expected around 8 December.

## What the bet is worth

VVV trades around **21 dollars** (21.21 on CoinGecko, 21.25 on DefiLlama at the time of writing), market cap just above one billion, fully diluted valuation 1.72 billion, up 10.6% over seven days and still 39% below the 34.53-dollar high of 21 September. Staking is worth 707.61 million, 67.9% of the market cap; TVL stays at zero by definition, because DefiLlama keeps staking as a separate series.

Onto these numbers Arthur Hayes has grafted his forecast, putting VVV among his "four horsemen" with a 40-dollar target, while declaring "small to medium" positions and no deadline. It is an opinion, not a datapoint.

The real question is another one: can a market of tasks turn a narrative token into a token with flows? The fee is tiny, the mechanism is transparent, and for the first time the burn depends not only on how many subscribers arrive, but on how many times someone uses someone else's work.
