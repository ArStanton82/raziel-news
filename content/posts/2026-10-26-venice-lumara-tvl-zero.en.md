Venice's weekend is made of two surfaces that rarely share a sentence: a film prize and a data page. On one side the platform presents itself as a production studio; on the other the market measures it with numbers that tell a much narrower story. The two are worth keeping apart, because adding them together is the easiest mistake available.

## The prize

The Lumara festival, presented by MoonPay and Venice, announced its category winners ahead of the evening of 26 October at the Roxy Hotel Cinema in New York. The Get Shorty Award, best film under two minutes, goes to ATO DE [LATER] by Luke Mazey, with 8,000 dollars and 800,000 Venice credits. Out of more than 700 submissions, six finalists and sixteen nominations: a marketing festival, certainly, but with a jury and a prize paid in credits that works as a measure of the ecosystem of the people who use the tools.

## The model that draws

On the same day Venice pushed a less spectacular and more concrete use of Kimi K3.1 Pro: mechanical parts, buildings, made-to-measure furniture "modelled to size", writing Blender and CAD scripts from a short description. The model is available in the anonymised tier, at 3 dollars per million input tokens and 15 output, with 20% off until 31 October. It is the kind of application that shifts perception: not a chatbot that talks about objects, but a program that parameterises them.

## How it is actually measured

Here the morning report and the primary page diverge, and the page wins. DefiLlama measures a TVL of **0 dollars** for Venice: the methodology counts only VVV tokens locked in the staking contract (sVVV) on Base. Staking is tracked as a separate series, not inside TVL: at the time of verification it stands at **849.77 million dollars**, **70.61%** of a market cap of 1.203 billion. The 1.74 billion figure going round this morning does not exist on that page: it is the improper sum of two different measures.

The rest of the numbers confirm the picture. Fees over the last 30 days are worth 1.01 million, cumulative fees 3.86 million, and they concern on-chain buy-and-burn only: subscriptions, API and credits settled in DIEM happen off-chain and DefiLlama does not see them. The token trades just above 24 dollars, roughly 29% below the high of 34.53 touched on 21 September.

The honest reading is that Venice is a company with an expanding product and a thin financial surface, almost three quarters of its market cap immobilised in staking and a minimal fraction of revenue verifiable on chain. This is not a judgement on value: it is the difference between what can be proven and what can be told.
