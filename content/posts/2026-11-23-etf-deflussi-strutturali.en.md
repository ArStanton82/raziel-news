The week from 16 to 20 November will close in the charts as one of the heaviest for US spot Bitcoin ETFs: about 1.79 billion dollars of net outflows, the fourth consecutive week in the red and the largest withdrawal since the week ending 26 June (SoSoValue, via The Block). The number invites a simple question: who is leaving, and why now.

## The number, broken down

One product did the bulk of it: BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust lost 1.37 billion during the week, Fidelity FBTC another 199 million. The funds' total net assets were 71.35 billion on Friday, down from about 110 billion in mid-September. It is worth keeping in mind: 1.79 billion out of 71.35 of assets is 2.5%, a bad week but not a flight.

## «More structural than in the past»

The most quoted reading comes from Dan Khus of LVRG Research: unlike previous waves, which were shorter and tied to single events, this four-week run «appears more structural», and coincides with sustained pressure on the price, heavy liquidations and a shift of institutional positions towards capital preservation. The analysts add the expectation of another Fed rate hike in December. It remains a hypothesis, not a datum.

## Meanwhile the price rebounds

The detail that escapes the outflow chronicles: the market has stopped falling. After dropping below 60,000 dollars on Friday, Bitcoin travels around 61,200-61,400 dollars with a gain of 2.5-3% over 24 hours (CoinGecko and Cointelegraph index), after an intraday low of 59,526. ETF outflows tell the flow; the price tells the balance between those selling inside the funds and those buying outside. For one week, the latter held.

## The night a bitcoin cost 17,000 dollars

One episode that concerns not the ETFs but the currency markets recalls how thin the scaffolding is. On Saturday, on Kraken's BTC/CAD pair, the price fell to 24,250 Canadian dollars in a fifteen-minute window: at the Bank of Canada reference rate, about 16,900 dollars, 71% below the 57,650 of the BTC/USD market in the same interval. Kraken said every order was executed correctly, that there is no evidence of breaches or compromised accounts, and that the trades remain valid. Adam Back hypothesised a market maker running dry on USD/CAD and arbitrage that propagated the misalignment; Kraken does not confirm.

## Two fragilities, not one

ETF outflows and the Canadian hole look like separate stories and yet say the same thing from two sides: the bitcoin market has become big in allocation and thin in mechanics. Almost two billion can be sold in a week without crashing the price, and at the same time a currency book can lose its reference and print a price that exists nowhere else. The question for the coming weeks is not whether the ETFs will buy again, but whether the liquidity under the price will be thick enough to withstand the next fear.
