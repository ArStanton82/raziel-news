Look at the price alone and the week looks flat. Look at where the dollars flow and it tells another story: capital coming back in, stablecoins turning into infrastructure, rules moving even without Congress.

## Capital returns, quietly

US spot bitcoin funds took in $134.5 million on 25 September, stretching the run to seven consecutive sessions above one hundred million and to almost $2.98 billion in total. Not a single outflow over the whole period. This is not euphoria: it is an orderly return after the outflows of mid-September.

## Stablecoins become rails

On 25 September the Circle Foundation announced two initiatives with the United Nations Development Programme and the World Food Programme: a "Digital Asset Innovation Pool" for programmatic payments, and funding for the infrastructure that stablecoin payments in humanitarian aid require. The same day, on Base, Coinbase's tokenised shares became collateral on Aave V4 to borrow USDC, with prices supplied by Chainlink. And Alpen announced that USDC is coming to Bitcoin, with no wrapped token, at mainnet launch. In the background, Brian Armstrong argues that crypto and stablecoins will be "the payment rail" for AI agents.

## Bitcoin under the hood

Vitalik Buterin recalled that PeerDAS has been running for almost a year "without notable issues" — the first large-scale case of data availability consensus in which no node downloads the whole block. It is the kind of news that does not move the price and changes the infrastructure.

## Washington, between reserves and rules

One clarification is needed here. Echo's report cites a post of 27 September in which Trump says his administration "is talking about" buying more bitcoin. News outlets, however, place the remark at the White House meeting of 19 August, not in a fresh statement: in late September the line resurfaced with no official act, no amounts and no dates. On the regulatory side, by contrast, the SEC Crypto Task Force's chief counsel, Taylor Lindman, explained that much can be done with existing authority — the "innovation exemption" — without waiting for the CLARITY Act, still stuck in the Senate.

Three different stories converge on one point: bitcoin and the digital dollar are ceasing to be separate worlds. The question is no longer whether the rails will exist, but who will control them.
