Erik Voorhees's answer to the accusations about Venice's privacy comes down to one sentence: "Any model marked Private is private. Private means zero logging of prompts and responses." It is a flat defence, and it moves the discussion from suspicion to semantics. Because the word "private", at Venice, is not a single promise: it is one tier out of four.

## What the rebuttal covers
According to The Defiant's account, the co-founder explained that some models in the Private band run on external servers under zero-retention agreements, and that the term "self-hosted" in the documents deserves to be made clearer. The company's privacy page confirms the arrangement: the Private tier, the default one, runs "on GPUs controlled by Venice or on partner infrastructure with zero-retention", with privacy "guaranteed by contractual commitments". The same page adds the caveat that rarely makes it into posts: it is "trust-based, depends on Venice and the partners honouring the contracts". Above it sit TEE, with hardware enclaves from NEAR AI Cloud and Phala verified by remote attestation, and E2EE, reserved for Pro subscribers. Below it sits Anonymous, where the provider "sees and probably retains" the prompt.

The rebuttal covers routing and logs. It does not cover the researcher's other objections — unencrypted tables, keys in the clear, models swapped out — which stay open, and which nobody has verified independently.

## Two hours, one token
On the market the answer was blunter than the words. On Wednesday evening, between 20:40 and 22:40 New York time, VVV went from 30.14 to 26.06 dollars, a −13.5% in two hours while bitcoin barely moved; then a bounce to 27.62. KuCoin speaks of a drop beyond 15%. Today the token is worth about 27 dollars, with a market cap around 1.33 billion: CoinGecko prints 26.98 and KuCoin 27.12, less than half a point apart. The 24-hour changes on the two listings point in opposite directions, so we don't publish them: the level is what stands.

## The cost of ambiguity
A tier that stands on a contract is a legitimate choice, declared and consistent with the costs of inference. The problem isn't the contract: it's calling it as though it were hardware. The only guarantees you can verify with your own means are TEE and E2EE, and they cost money. For everything else the user has to believe a vendor and its partners, which is exactly what the word "private" promised to free them from.

That the community is not running away is what venicestats' weekly Buzz Board says: 1,655 posts from 980 accounts between 20 and 27 November, 518 accounts never seen before talking about Venice, 1.86 million views. It is a third-party board with its own formula, not a census; but it is enough to say the discussion is alive. The point, now, isn't whether Venice is private. It's at which of the four tiers, and who certifies it.
