There is a scene that has been repeating for weeks, and this week it produced its sharpest photograph. The VVV token closed at 20.19 dollars, down 11.7%; over the same stretch the treasury spent 237,711 dollars on gradual purchases, taking the current buyback cycle to 533,464 dollars, double the 269,061 spent across all of October. The price falls and the machine that buys accelerates. That is not a paradox: it is the design.

## The buyback doesn't follow the price, it follows revenue

The mechanism is verifiable on-chain: Venice funds a multisig in USDC, CoW Protocol spends it in small batches, and around the 7th-9th of the following month the Safe burns the accumulated VVV. After a 200,000 dollar transfer on November 2, daily spending went from about 20,000 to about 34,500 dollars. The current cycle has accumulated 23,439 VVV and exceeds every completed cycle, including the August peak of 321,830 dollars.

The other side is arithmetic: on a constant budget, a lower price burns more tokens. The November burn was expected around the 8th and had not yet landed; projections put it between 485 and 678 thousand dollars, that is between 24,073 and 33,662 VVV. Anyone reading these numbers as a sign of the token's strength is aiming at the wrong target: they are a photograph of revenue, not of a quotation.

## Usage grows where the premium thins

On the demand side, DIEM utilisation rose to 49.5% of staking capacity, up 4.3 points and above the quarterly baseline of 45.4%. It means stakers' inference capacity is being consumed for almost half its size, with about 14,500 dollars of value served per day. DIEM's market price, however, fell 6.8% to 1,624 dollars: the credit costs less, and that makes minting less attractive than utilisation alone suggests.

The other indicators describe an activity stabilising rather than slowing: 21,101 new subscriptions (-2.9%, the second decline after the late-October peak) and credit purchases of 2.145 million (-1.6% after seven weeks of growth). Net staking flow is negative by 525,256 VVV, almost all of it from a single 500,000 token unstake.

## The catalogue, meanwhile, gets wider

In the same window Venice added three model families: Krea 3, with instruction-guided editing and up to eight reference images at 4K output starting from 0.07 dollars per image; Kuaishou's Kling 4.0 Flash, stated at 40% less per clip than Kling 4.0, with native audio up to 20 seconds; and the GPT Image 2.6 variants, Flare and Sunburst, both up to 4K.

A catalogue widening while the token falls is consistent with the underlying thesis: the value would sit in usage, not in scarcity. On DefiLlama Venice's TVL is zero by methodology: the 709.24 million dollars in staking, 71.58% of market capitalisation, are a separate series. Anyone adding the two line items is measuring two different things.
