A repost that went viral last night claims the Netherlands wants to tax unrealised Bitcoin gains and that an official government proposal would hit self-custody wallets. The story is more interesting than that, and it goes the other way.

## What the bill actually says

There is, to be precise, a bill: the Wet werkelijk rendement box 3, approved by the House of Representatives on 12 February 2026. It would replace the presumed-returns system with a 36 per cent tax on wealth growth, including annual changes in the value of shares, bonds and cryptocurrencies. In theory a Dutch resident holding Bitcoin would pay tax on a gain never cashed in, with a reduced exemption threshold and losses carried forward to later years.

## The fact-check: the viral post tells the story backwards

Here the sources diverge from the post. The text is stuck in the Senate, which postponed the vote after 36 pages of questions and a petition with more than 61,000 signatures. Since Budget Day in September the government has confirmed the change of course: according to Dutch press reports and Bloomberg, the tax on unrealised gains will be dropped, at least for shares and holdings, and replaced by a model that taxes gains only at the moment of sale. The exemption threshold would fall from 1,800 to 1,000 euros. The bill is therefore not "an official government proposal" hitting wallets: it is a law approved by the House that the government is in effect dismantling. Part of the trade press, on 29 September, still presents it as a live proposal awaiting the Senate: the viral wording is a delay, not an invention.

## Bitcoin at $83,000, between whales and the halving

The rest of the day offers more solid material. Strive accumulated 6,106 BTC between 24 August and 25 September, for about 491 million dollars at an average price of $80,375: a figure confirmed on-chain and by the trade press, which takes the company to around 27,400 BTC. On prices, Bitcoin travels around $83,000 and analysts point to the 82,000-82,500 area as the level bulls must defend. More uncertain is the claim that whales bought more than 41,000 BTC in ten days: Santiment data confirm an accumulation of about 41,000 BTC, but over a two-week window, not ten days, and the "six-week high" finds no support. The halving countdown, by contrast, is consistent: 564 days according to CoinWarz, with the event estimated for 15 April 2028.

## Conclusion

The lesson of the day is not about the merits of Dutch tax policy, which remains a legitimate and still open choice. It is about how slowly the crypto narrative absorbs facts: a post with 181 reposts describes a tax as imminent while the government that proposed it is taking it apart. In a market that lives on narratives, the difference between a story and a headline is often just a week's delay.
